Curatorial Research Trips — January–June 2027

Prize £1,000 research grant

Fluxus Art Projects funds work that moves between the two countries it exists to connect: it supports projects promoting the contemporary French art scene in the UK and the contemporary British art scene in France, and it runs its own call for applications twice a year. This round covers projects taking place between January and June 2027 and closes at midnight on Tuesday 24 November 2026. The call has two separate streams and the difference matters before you start an application. Exhibition projects are funded between £1,000 and £10,000, but the fund states that applications must be submitted by the organising institution or association and not by the artists themselves — eligible applicants there are non-profit art centres, museums, cultural institutions, research centres, online projects and artists' publications — so an individual artist cannot apply to that stream. Curatorial research trips work the other way round: applications must be made directly by the curators, and the grant is a flat £1,000 per trip. The curatorial criteria are specific. You must be French or resident in France for at least five years, or British or resident in the UK for at least five years; you need a minimum of three years of professional experience, or evidence of significant professional practice within an art organisation or as an independent curator in the field of contemporary art; and if you are attached to an institution you must supply its authorisation to travel for the research you are proposing. Fluxus describes its field as visual arts in the broadest sense across all disciplines, and the curatorial criteria name contemporary art, so a photography curator is in scope; the fund says it will prioritise innovative practices and the production of new works. The call page names no application fee at any point. Applications go through a Google Form and must be written in English, and the fund asks you to send a confirmation email to its own address as well as submitting the form. Fluxus Art Projects is a Franco-British fund supported by the Institut français du Royaume-Uni, the British Council, Arts Council England, Creative Scotland and the French Ministry of Culture; it also runs the Magnetic artist residencies in tandem pairs of a French region with a UK nation, and prizes at Frieze London and Art Basel Paris, all of which are closed at the time of writing. Applications are selected by the fund's standing Artistic Committee, named on its About page: Hélène Nguyen-Ban as chair, Margot Heller of the South London Gallery, Caroline Bourgeois of the Bourse du Commerce, Wells Fray-Smith of the Barbican, Daria de Beauvais of the Palais de Tokyo, Martin Clark of Camden Art Centre, Nicolas Gausserand of the musées d'Orsay and the Orangerie, and Edward Gillman of Chisenhale Gallery, alongside representatives of the public partners. The fund publishes no notification date for this round.
