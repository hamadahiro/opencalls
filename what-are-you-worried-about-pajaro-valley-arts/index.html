What Are You Worried About?

Pajaro Valley Arts, the Watsonville nonprofit, invites “artists living and/or working in the greater Bay Area” to an invitational and juried exhibition curated by journalist and artist Lillian Schroth. The brief is the worry itself: “Whatever keeps you up at night has a place in this show” — from money, relationships, kids, insomnia, anxiety and “paranoia about what other people think” out to “your city, your country, or the world — AI, the economy, wealth inequality, immigrant rights … climate change, the wars in Gaza, Ukraine and Iran”. PVA states “all genres — and worries — accepted” and “we welcome all art forms and mediums”, listing painting, sculpture, collage, video, audio and writing, so a photographer enters on the same footing as any other medium; each entrant also writes a short description of how the work connects to the theme, and that text hangs with the work if selected. Entry takes up to 4 JPEGs of work new to PVA (no larger than 300 dpi and 6” in any direction, filenames as surname plus title), with writing capped at 300 words and audio or video at 3 minutes for the submission — a selected time-based work may run longer. The calendar is published in full: jurors review 1–15 December, artists are notified on 17 December 2026, loan agreements are due 23 December, work is delivered to Watsonville on 26 January 2027, and the show runs 9 February – 13 March 2027 with a reception on Saturday 13 February, 1–3pm. THE COSTS: there is no entry fee, but “upon acceptance/selection into the exhibit, there is a $35.00 participation fee for each artist, to be paid with the submittal of the loan agreement”, which PVA says “helps offset exhibit expenses” while noting “some participation sponsorships may be available” — ask if the fee is a barrier. Sales commission is 30% for PVA members and 50% for non-members, and the page asks for membership “in good standing through March 13, 2026” (the organiser’s own date, which sits a year before this exhibition closes). Delivery is the artist’s own job and PVA states “we are unable to accept artwork through the mail for this exhibit”, so entering means getting the work to Watsonville in person (or by a representative) in the 10am–2pm window on 26 January. Framed work must be wired and ready to hang, and each piece is insured to a maximum of $2,500.
