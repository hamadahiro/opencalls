Color Effect

OCCCA — an artist-run non-profit in the Santa Ana Artists Village, serving the community since 1980 — asks artists to treat colour as a force rather than decoration: something “coded with history, desire, identity, power, memory, and resistance” that “divides and unites, seduces, disrupts, obscures, and reveals.” The call wants ambitious proposals that embrace tension and contradiction, and names the practices it is looking for: “painting, installation, sculpture, photography, performance, video, sound, textiles, or interdisciplinary forms.” Juror Rachel Adams is Chief Curator and Director of Programs at the Bemis Center for Contemporary Arts, and has previously held roles at University at Buffalo Art Galleries, Oregon Contemporary and The Contemporary Austin; her curatorial practice sits at the intersections of photography, installation, sound, performance, video, new media and architecture. The call is open to all artists without regional restriction. Up to nine pieces may be entered — $45 covers the first three, then $15 per additional work. Accepted artists are notified January 8, 2027; the exhibition opens February 6, 2027 with a second reception on March 6 and closes March 20. Artwork is capped at 50 inches in any dimension including framing and 25 pounds, and OCCCA retains a 40% commission on sales.
