Man or Bear: No Safe Choice

Prize Group exhibition

Woman Made Gallery takes the viral question — alone in the woods, would you rather meet a man or a bear — and builds a spring exhibition out of what it exposed. The call is explicit about its subject: “male violence and abuse against women” as it is organised and amplified online, from “the undercurrents of red-pilling young boys and online stalking” to “AI-produced revenge porn, the trans-vestigation of female athletes, manosphere gaming culture, and disinformation smear campaigns of women in positions of power”. Its closing line sets the tone of what the gallery wants: “Both are wild, but at least a woman will be believed when she is attacked by a bear.” The published areas of inquiry are deliberately wide — critique of the manosphere, incel culture, online gender investigations targeting trans people, frat and rape culture, “reclamation of authorship, narrative power, and cultural agency”, intersectional experience across gender, race, class, sexuality, disability and migration, and art as a site of resistance — and the gallery asks for “your most raw reactions over a plethora of mediums and forms”. Submissions are open to women and nonbinary artists at all career stages “working in any medium”; stills and moving image are both accepted at upload. Selection is by MA students from the School of the Art Institute of Chicago rather than a single named juror. Entry is $30 for one to three works, with up to two entries per artist, and WMG publishes reduced fees and fee waivers reserved for BIPOC and LGBTQIA+ artists on request. Work previously shown at WMG is not eligible, nothing may exceed 6 feet on its widest side or 75 pounds unless the artist handles delivery and pickup personally, artists arrange and pay for shipping to the gallery, and sales carry a 40% commission.
