Prize Solo online exhibition

L.A. Photo Curator's calls are online by design and the platform is upfront about it, which makes them easy to price. Every entrant goes into the group exhibition with an artist statement, biography and website link, so nobody pays $25 and gets nothing; the competitive part is the first place, which brings a solo online exhibition, an article with a Q&A built from the juror's responses, and a month on the platform's splash page. Selected work also stays in play for the Top 40 Images of the Year and the Photographer of the Year title. Judging here is Laurie Freitag, who has run the platform for eleven years, was named one of ten Incredible Women Photographers to Follow in 2026, and has recently sold work through Duncan Miller Gallery.

The theme is unfashionable and honest about it. Freitag's brief leans on the difference between an animal you look after and a companion who has taken up space in your life — the greeting at the door, the following from room to room, the small rhythms. Anatole France's line about no soul being awakened until one has loved an animal heads the prospectus. For a photographer with a body of work about their own household rather than about a subject at arm's length, this is a rare place to put it.

Mechanics are low-tech: hit the Buy Now button to pay $25 by PayPal or card for either three or six images, then email exactly that number of JPEGs as a ZIP — 1024 pixels on the long side, 72 DPI, under 1 MB each, named First_Last_Title.jpg — with your statement and biography in the body of the message, your website and Instagram written out in full, and the PayPal transaction ID and payer name included. Entry fees are non-refundable and late work is not eligible. Five per cent of entry fees is donated to a charity the winner chooses. One entrant is also invited to guest-jure a future L.A. Photo Curator call, which is a genuinely useful thing to have on a CV. Deadline 27 September 2026.
