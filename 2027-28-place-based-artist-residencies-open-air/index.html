2027/28 Place-Based Artist Residencies

Prizes 4–6 week residency $35/day stipend Housing Studio

Open AIR is a Missoula-based non-profit that runs place-based artist residencies across Montana, partly funded by the Montana Arts Council and four family foundations, and it is taking applications for its 2027/28 cycle of residencies the organisers bill as four to six weeks long, though the published session dates run from three weeks in the Selway Bitterroot wilderness to six in Butte. Rather than a single studio building, the programme places artists at specific sites - the published 2027/28 list is Garden City Harvest, Home ReSource, the Daly Mansion, the Flathead Lake Biological Station, Rocky Mountain Gardens, Philipsburg, the spectrUM science museum, the Selway Bitterroot Frank Church wilderness, Missoula Public Library, Butte and a natural-history residency run with the Montana Natural History Center and the University of Montana's zoology museum - and asks them to make work in relation to that place: "artists can connect with the individuals stewarding that site and develop unique work in relation to the place they are in residence". Applicants choose between three and five sites from the published list, which is split into general residency sites and a separate set of Indigenous residency opportunities sharing the same deadline: a five-site summer cohort (American Prairie, the Kingsley History Project, the Montana Heritage Center, the Moon-Randolph Homestead, Five Valleys Land Trust) and a fall residency at the CSKT Bison Range, both open to Indigenous artists only. There is no charge to take part once selected. Open AIR provides studio space at the site, housing for every artist travelling from out of town, community engagement and professional-development opportunities including exhibitions and artist talks, a listing and promotion on its own channels, and a modest stipend of 35 US dollars for every day of the residency; artists pay their own travel and food. In return, residents join weekly check-ins, take part in community engagement such as studio visits, talks, performances or workshops shaped around their own practice, share their process in photographs and recorded talks, and contribute one piece of digital content or artwork to the organisation's collection as an educational resource - a physical object is optional, not required. Artists in Missoula also get access to community resources including the Montana Natural History Center, Home ReSource, Free Cycles, the MCAT Media Center, MUD Missoula, Missoula Public Library and the University of Montana. The call is open to artists of all disciplines living and working in the United States and abroad, aged 18 and over, at any career stage, and students may apply for a residency during a break; Indigenous artists and members of other under-represented groups are explicitly encouraged. Photography is well represented in the programme's own history: past cohorts list Jordan Lefler and Logan Lefler (photographer/filmmaker) and Forest Woodward (photographer) at the CSKT Bison Range, and the Belgian photographer and visual artist Catherine Lemblé in Missoula. Applications cost 30 dollars for up to three sites and 10 dollars for each additional site, and the organisers waive the fee on request if it is a barrier. The application asks for five to ten work samples (or adequate samples for non-visual media, no file over 1 GB), a 100-word bio, a 500-word description of your creative practice, a creative-practice resume, responses on why your chosen sites fit your interests, and optionally a workshop idea for the community. A team of unnamed jurors selects on merit, excellence and fit to site, reading work samples alongside the site commentary; finalists are interviewed by phone. The deadline is 11:59 pm MST on Monday 30 November 2026 and everyone is notified by the end of March 2027.
