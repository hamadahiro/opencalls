Long River Review 2027 — Art & Photography

Prize Publication (print)

Long River Review is UConn's literary and arts magazine, edited by an MFA-student board and now on its twenty-ninth print issue, and its art section is a straightforward, free route into print for photographers early in their career. The magazine's stated aim is to amplify voices “drowned out by other voices, or voices that might not otherwise have been heard”, and the art call welcomes all mediums — photography, illustration, printmaking, painting, comics, drawing, mixed media — rather than ranking them. There is no theme and no fee.

The submission limit is five to ten pieces, each accompanied by a 150-word artist statement. Production specifications are print-first and unusually precise: TIFF or EPS files at 350 dpi, between 5.75 inches minimum and 8 inches maximum width, CMYK for colour work and greyscale for black and white — greyscale here meaning complex tonal gradation, not simply the absence of colour. Only unpublished work is accepted. Simultaneous submissions are fine provided you tell the editors if the work is taken elsewhere. Accepted contributors grant first North American serial rights, after which rights revert to them, and receive two copies of the issue; there is no payment.

Two things to know. Cash prizes exist — the Gloriana Gill Art Awards and the Long River Art Award — but the guidelines are explicit that they apply to art submitted by UConn students, so for outside photographers the return is publication and copies rather than money. And the magazine has a hard AI policy: it will not publish writing or art with AI-generated content, and the guidelines set out the process the editors follow on suspicion, including asking for original files or earlier drafts. A submitter who is honest about AI use has the piece declined and may submit again; one who denies it is banned permanently. The reading period runs 15 September to 15 December 2026, with responses by the end of April.
