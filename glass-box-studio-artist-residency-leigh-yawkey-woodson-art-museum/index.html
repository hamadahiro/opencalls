Glass Box Studio Artist Residency

Prize Residency with housing, honorarium and travel reimbursement

The Leigh Yawkey Woodson Art Museum in Wausau, Wisconsin runs a residency in its Glass Box Studio, a working studio on the museum campus that the public can see into. The museum's own words on eligibility are that visual artists at any stage of their career and working in any medium are welcome, and that the studio exists for artists to experiment, explore and produce individually developed or collaborative artwork in any medium — the museum publishes no medium list and names no exclusion for this programme. One caution worth reading before you apply, because it sits on the very same page: the museum's flagship annual exhibition Birds in Art explicitly bars photography and documentary work, and its no-AI rule belongs to that exhibition. Neither applies to the residency, which is a separate call with its own application portal and its own payment page. Residencies run from a few days up to one month and the applicant proposes the length and dates. Residents get 24/7 access to the Glass Box Studio (WiFi, drop-down electricity, slop sink, large work tables and stools, a cork wall for hanging, plus access to a wood shop, power tools, a small etching press and a kiln), housing in a one-bedroom house on the museum campus, an honorarium set to match the length and programming of each residency, reimbursement for travel, mileage and meals, and materials for any workshops they teach. In exchange, residents keep open studio hours at least two days a week during museum hours, and take part in outreach or educational programming — including, if they wish, sessions with audiences experiencing memory loss, low vision or blindness, and with children aged five and under. Applicants must be over 21 and living and working in the United States. The application asks for a 300-word artist statement, a 500-word description of the proposed project, a 300-word statement of goals, an account of any teaching experience, a CV and a portfolio of 10 to 15 works. Museum staff review the applications; top applicants are invited to a virtual studio visit in mid-November before invitations go out in late November. Admission to the museum is always free.
