København Noir — Din By i Sort-Hvid

Prizes DKK 2,000 in CEWE products Exhibition at Absalon, 22 November

A free black-and-white photo hunt through Copenhagen's old working-class districts, run by Copenhagen Photo Festival with Københavns Museum, CEWE and a group of Copenhagen photographers under the city's Vinter i København programme. Nine archive photographs from the museum's collection have been installed outdoors on the streets and squares of Nørrebro, Vesterbro and Nordvest — each one at the spot where it was originally taken. The task is to find them, one or two or all nine, and submit your own black-and-white pictures made in the area around them. The festival's framing is unapologetically atmospheric: a tribute to contrast, to the aesthetics of decay and renewal, to street life and dusk, to "that Copenhagen which is rarely captured", and to the time of year Danes associate with endless darkness — the page opens with Henrik Nordbrandt's line that the year has sixteen months, "November, December, January... November, November, November, November". Street photography in black and white is what the organisers say they are after, worked alone or with others. Entry is free and every submitted photograph is automatically in the running to be exhibited at Absalon on 22 November, the day the project culminates at the festival's off-season photo market. Three guided photo walks with the photographers Christopher 'Flip' Egeberg, Jón Bjarni Hjartarson and Christine Lorenzen run alongside the hunt, offering black-and-white street technique to anyone who wants it. The entry terms, which live on the CEWE contest platform rather than on the festival's own page, restrict entry to people resident in Copenhagen ("Deltagelsesberettigede er fysiske personer, som har bopæl i København"), and the work has to be made around the nine specific locations, so this is for people who live in the city and are in it before 3 November; entrants under 18 confirm that a parent or guardian has read the terms and consents. The same terms set out what is actually awarded: the contest runs in nine categories, one per location, with a maximum of one photograph per category; a specialist jury picks 27 winning images (three per location) for the Absalon show and ranks the best three, where first prize is CEWE photo products worth DKK 2,000, second is Christopher 'Flip' Egeberg's photobook about the street artist SPYO, given by Københavns Museum, and third is a voucher for two tickets to Copenhagen Photo Festival 2027. Winners are told by email, with no notification date published, and nothing is posted out — prizes are handed over at Absalon on 22 November or collected afterwards from the festival office. Images made wholly by computer or by artificial intelligence are barred and will be excluded, retouching that adds or removes content is not allowed, and the extent of any editing has to be stated in the image description. The festival calls it a taste of next year's edition, when the hunt returns at larger scale as Copenhagen Photo Festival moves from June to November.
