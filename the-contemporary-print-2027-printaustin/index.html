The Contemporary Print 2027

Prizes $500 Exhibition

This is a printmaking exhibition, and the honest version of the eligibility rule is the first thing a photographer needs: an inkjet print or a silver gelatin print will not get in. PrintAustin states it twice, on its own open-calls page and in the platform prospectus, in identical words — “Solely digital and photographic prints are not eligible.”

What does get in is photopolymer. PrintAustin lists “photopolymer plate (i.e. SolarPlates)” among the six traditional print media it accepts as a primary technique, alongside lithography, relief, intaglio, silkscreen and monotype, and it accepts digital or non-traditional output as a print *element* so long as the primary technique is a print one. For anyone making SolarPlate etchings or photopolymer photogravures from their own negatives or digital files, that is an explicit invitation — and a rare one, since most alternative-process shows are run by photography organisations rather than by printmakers with a museum curator judging.

The money is modest and the structure is unusually decent about it. Thirty-five dollars covers three pieces, PrintAustin members pay ten, each piece beyond three adds ten up to a maximum of six, and the organiser publishes a hardship waiver available to anyone regardless of membership. Best in Show is $500 cash plus a recorded podcast interview with Hello, Print Friend. Sales are consigned 70/30 in the artist's favour and the gallery insures the work and carries the risk while it holds it. Shipping the artist arranges and pays for in both directions, with a prepaid return label required before the work will be hung — but PrintAustin puts a stipend of at least $50 toward it, which a show at this fee level usually does not.

One discrepancy to hold loosely: the organiser's own page gives the exhibition as 19 January to 11 March 2027 with a reception on 27 February, while the platform prospectus says 21 January to 20 March 2027 with the reception listed as TBD. The deadline is not in dispute — both surfaces resolve to the end of 31 October 2026. The date your work has to arrive is a three-way split inside the prospectus itself: 9 December in the terms and conditions, “in December” in the shipping block, and “JAN 9 Artwork due” in the organiser's own IMPORTANT DATES table. Take the delivery date and the show dates from PrintAustin's acceptance email, not from any one of those lines.
