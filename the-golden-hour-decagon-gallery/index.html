The Golden Hour

Decagon Gallery's Flash calls are the fee-free half of its programme — internally curated thematic exhibitions with no entry fee, no awards and no rankings — and THE GOLDEN HOUR is the next one: low, warm, fleeting light and the brief moments it makes. The gallery is after what that light does to a photograph and what a photograph does with it, and it says plainly that it is less interested in the most dramatic sunset than in the image that makes you feel the time of day. The obvious version counts — a street turned to copper, a face lit from the side, a field burning amber for ten minutes before it goes grey — but so does the quieter one: the last light on a kitchen wall, a familiar room looking strange for a moment, something you nearly missed because the light was already going. Looser readings are invited too. A golden hour can be a peak moment, a short window of grace, a time you did not know mattered until it was over; if the photograph is about that fleeting, luminous quality, it belongs in the call. All genres and processes are welcome — landscape, street, portrait, still life, documentary, constructed, abstract, film, digital and alternative process, colour or black and white. Selected photographs are presented together as an online exhibition at Decagon Gallery from 15 February to 15 April 2027, with notification on 8 February. The juror is John Manno, the gallery's founder and director. Entry is open to photographers 18 and older anywhere in the world: up to four photographs, JPGs at least 2,100 pixels on the long side, sRGB, with no text, watermarks or borders, named FirstName-LastName_Title.jpg. Photographs must be your own work and AI-generated images are not accepted. Photographers keep all rights including copyright; selection grants the gallery permission to show and promote the work, always with credit. Decagon is a Brooklyn photography platform founded by John Manno and guided by curator and artistic director Teona Machavariani.
