Premier Prix photographique du Musée Postal

Prize €15,000 production grant

The subject is narrow and the museum says so plainly: the project has to be about La Poste as it is now. Its own examples — mobility and flows, ecological transformation, technological innovation, territorial presence, trades and social questions — read as an invitation to treat a national postal service as a way into contemporary French labour and geography rather than as a nostalgia brief. Every photographic register is admitted, documentary through experimental, and the project is expected to continue the practice you already have rather than depart from it.

What the €15,000 buys is a commission, not a reward for finished work: the grant is earmarked for making the project, production runs February to June 2027, the prints are made in July 2027 by La Fabrique de l'image in Ardèche at the prize's expense, and a selection of signed and numbered prints then enters a collection of 250,000 photographic documents. The winner also gets access and support inside the La Poste group to make the work happen, which for a subject like this is worth as much as the money.

Two clauses in the règlement are binding and easy to miss. AI use must be declared explicitly in the project description or statement of intent, with the tools named and the extent of their use given as far as possible; not declaring it can disqualify you even after you have been named winner. And the grant is only paid once you sign a copyright assignment contract covering the works produced — the contract fixes the rights assigned, their duration and territory, and the winner assigns the museum non-exclusive reproduction and representation rights; the règlement states that the winner's fee may include, in whole or in part, payment for those rights.
