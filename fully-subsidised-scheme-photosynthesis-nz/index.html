Fully Subsidised Scheme — Free Studio and Darkroom Access

Prizes Fully subsidised studio and darkroom access Paid mentors

Photosynthesis, the non-profit that runs a community photography studio and darkroom in Ōtautahi Christchurch, covers the full cost of its space and equipment for qualifying projects. The scheme is funded by the Rātā Foundation and grew out of a 2023 pilot. Its current document covers 2025/26 and states that it is open for applications, with no closing date. The subsidy covers every cost of using the studio and darkroom and their equipment, for shooting, workshops, meetings, mentoring or learning, as long as the use belongs to the approved project. Participants receive keys, against a one-time bond, and can use the spaces 24 hours a day, seven days a week, booked through the organisation's portal and subject to availability. Photosynthesis finds mentors for the project and pays them. Nothing else is funded: artist fees, printing, publication and exhibition costs stay with the artist, who may look for outside funding. Projects are judged on four criteria. The work gives a voice to people traditionally under-represented in fine art, such as sexual and gender minorities, ethnic and cultural minorities, and disabled and elderly communities. It could not go ahead without the subsidy. It needs the studio and equipment many times, not once. It ends in published work an audience can see, such as a book or zine, an exhibition of prints or a video online. The scheme is for local artists. Past projects include Life/Styles 2024, a photographic archive made with young people from across the city and shown at Tūranga library, and Mau Rākau, a book and exhibition about practitioners of Māori weaponry. Applicants fill in an expression-of-interest form covering who is involved, the project's length and output, its current status and how it meets the criteria. Staff then arrange a meeting at the studio and work through approval with the applicant. There is no formal reporting. Projects that do not qualify may be offered reduced non-commercial rates instead.
