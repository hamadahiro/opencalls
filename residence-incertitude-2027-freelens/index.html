Résidence Incertitude 2027

Prizes €6,000 4–6 week residency €3,000 expenses Book publication Exhibition

Freelens — the French photographers' association recognised as being of public utility — and IRIT, the CNRS computer-science research institute in Toulouse, run this annual photographic residency on the theme of uncertainty in science. It is conceived and directed by Cédric Févotte, CNRS research director at IRIT, with artistic direction by Céline Pévrier of sun/sun éditions. One photographer is invited to build a body of work across four to six weeks at IRIT, taken in one to three stays in the first half of 2027, with weekly check-ins and introductions to researchers whose expertise fits the project; applicants are asked not to approach IRIT scientists before applying. The laureate receives €6,000 inclusive of charges and VAT (of which €1,000 is the rights assignment for the presentation, exhibition and book), paid 40% on signature, 30% after the work with the publisher and 30% after the work with the exhibition venue, plus up to €3,000 for travel, accommodation in Toulouse and technical costs. On top of that sit a dedicated envelope for sun/sun éditions to publish a book of the project and a production budget for the resulting exhibition at Galerie Le Château d'Eau, the photography venue in Toulouse, alongside a public presentation at IRIT. The call is open to professional photographers — photographic authors or visual artists using the medium — French and international, who are fluent in French; the residency is open to pluridisciplinary projects that weave writing, sound or other modes into the photography. Applying is free but the règlement requires every applicant to join Freelens for €15 by card or PayPal, a membership that also covers every other Freelens event, grant and prize. The dossier is a single PDF of at most 20 pages and 10 MB: an intent note of up to five pages, a portfolio of up to fifteen, the proposed residency calendar, a one-page CV, a short bio and the completed application form, filed through the Google form by midnight on 30 November 2026. A pre-jury of Freelens board members and the residency's direction shortlists five candidates for a video interview before the final jury, which the règlement fixes at five seats — Févotte, Pévrier, Freelens president Sophie Knittel, one figure from the photography sector and one from scientific research. Interviews are scheduled for 17 December, subject to change, with the result announced "le 23 décembre 2027 au plus tard" as the page prints it. The 2026 laureate was Éléa-Jeanne Schmitter, and the residency carries the French Ministry of Culture's Bicentenaire de la Photographie label through 30 September 2027.
