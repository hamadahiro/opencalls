The Scenic Route: Reimagining Highway 250

Prize $500–$1,500 project grant

Project grants of $500–$1,500 for artist-led activations of downtown Lanesboro, a small Minnesota town in Fillmore County whose main street — Highway 250 — is dug up through 2027–28 for water, sewer, roadway, sidewalk, lighting and trail work. Lanesboro Arts, which has worked in the town for over four decades and whose city council in 2014 declared the whole town an Arts Campus, is funding between 10 and 20 projects to "change the narrative from one of uncertainty to one of opportunity" while the construction runs. WHAT COUNTS AS A PROJECT: "their own artistic activation of the space, whether through installation, performance, or other creative programming" — the organisation's own examples run from "creating an art installation, hosting an artful picnic, throwing a dance party, offering a new point of view on construction, or something else creative". Photography is not named as a medium on either surface and the award is for a public activation rather than for a body of pictures; the organisation's own gallery calls do state "All visual arts media are eligible". A photographer's route in is therefore a project sited downtown: documenting the dig and the people living through it, a street-level installation of prints, a participatory portrait session, a projection. Proposals are judged on their potential to engage Lanesboro, logistical feasibility, and fit with four stated goals — connection, beauty and pride downtown; visitors and residents stopping, shopping and lingering; collaboration across sectors amid the disruption; and conversation about construction, maintenance and building Lanesboro's future. WHO CAN APPLY: "the application is open to artists and community members. An established art practice is not required." There is no geographic bar, but "Priority will be given to residents of Fillmore County, as well as BIPOC, LGBTQIA+, and/or disabled individuals" — and the work has to happen in downtown Lanesboro between April and October 2027, which is the practical limit. Free to apply, by a form on the organisation's own site asking for a brief project proposal with intended location, audience, goals and timeline. The priority deadline is 15 January 2027 at 11:59 pm; after that "Rolling applications will be accepted until project funds are depleted". Lanesboro Arts runs supporting workshops around the call, and the application page says applicants are "highly encouraged" to have attended one of the Civic Arts Engagement Workshops: a virtual Civic Arts Engagement Workshop with Springboard for the Arts on 12 November, Creative Idea Development round tables on 22 October and 13 December at the Sylvan Brewing Scale Room, and Creative Project Support round tables on 18 February and 14 March 2027. The partners named are the Minnesota Department of Transportation and UMN Extension. PAID TO APPLY: the application page states that "the first fifty applicants who submit a full, completed application will receive a $75 seed stipend, in recognition of the time, energy, and care that went into the application process", offered by Lanesboro Arts itself. AI: Lanesboro Arts policy is that "artwork created with the use of generative AI programs is not accepted in the Sales or Exhibition Galleries, nor is it considered for project support", and the form carries a required affirmation that the proposal "does not and will not involve the use of generative AI".
