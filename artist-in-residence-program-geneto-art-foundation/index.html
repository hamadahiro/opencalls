Artist-in-Residence Program

Prizes 60-day Kyoto residency Exhibition

A Kyoto residency run by the GENETO ART FOUNDATION in ART COTTAGE no.2, a machiya townhouse about a hundred years old near Higashiyama Sanjō whose interior was stripped back to the original beams and rebuilt in white — "architecture as a vessel for artworks", with the ground floor intended as a studio and the upper floor as living space, though the foundation says the use of the building is left to the artist. The current cycle is held with Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei, the Tendai temple complex that is a UNESCO World Heritage Site: artists make new work while spending time with the temple's Buddhist philosophy and practice, and the finished work is then shown at Enryaku-ji itself for a limited period. The foundation takes about three artists a year for stays of up to 60 days; accommodation is free for the whole period and the studio and facilities come with it, alongside promotional support through the GENETO Group's network and exhibition or event programming shaped around the individual artist. What the artist pays is travel and production costs, both stated plainly as the artist's own, plus a non-refundable ¥5,000 application fee that the foundation says buys the administrative and review process and guarantees nothing. There is no application deadline: applications are read on a rolling basis from anywhere in the world, with the foundation's own framing that it invites artists from overseas to Kyoto, and across all career stages. The residency page sets out the procedure — prepare the documents, pay the fee, then "submit the application documents together with proof of payment" — but publishes no address to send them to: the only mailbox on the cottage site is the one its contact page offers "for questions or inquiries", and the foundation's own directory listing, which asks for an artistic background, a portfolio of recent work and a proposal describing the intended research or project, publishes a general address of its own. Nothing further is charged if you are chosen: that listing states there is no residency participation fee for selected artists and no cash stipend, with travel, materials, meals, insurance and personal costs the artist's. The people printed at the foot of the page are the foundation's officers, not a jury — Enryaku-ji's head of religious education as chairperson, its worship-department director and the deputy director of Enryakuji Kaikan as directors, GENETO GROUP's founder as director, Art Fair Tokyo's CEO as special advisor and LensG's founder as curator and director — and the only thing the page says about choosing artists is that the foundation curates. Photography is one of the disciplines the foundation lists as accepted, and the programme has run before: seven past residents are profiled, among them a photographer working in large-format sheet film and an artist who builds digital collages from her own Kyoto photographs, and the work made in one of those stays was shown at Enryakuji Kaikan on Mount Hiei as "Architecture of Memory".
