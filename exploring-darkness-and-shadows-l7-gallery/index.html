Exploring Darkness and Shadows

Prizes $300 Exhibition

L7 Gallery, a Bellingham, Washington space that shows neo-figurative, neo-expressionist, spiritual-abstraction and outsider work, invites international artists to its next online exhibition on the many meanings of darkness and shadow. The call sets the theme against the usual readings of fear, chaos and death: the night sky, fertile soil and the maternal womb are all dark places necessary for life. It leans on Jun’ichirō Tanizaki’s In Praise of Shadows — Eastern cultures preferring jade’s pensive luster to a shallow brilliance, tarnished silver over polish, aged grain over new wood — and on Romeo seeing Juliet hang upon the night like a jewel in an Ethiope’s ear, and it ranges its references from da Vinci, Vermeer and Goya to Vija Celmins, Astro Boy and Kara Walker. The gallery supplies definitions of skiagraphia, tenebrism, chiaroscuro and sfumato as starting points. Photography is named among the accepted media, alongside painting, drawing, watercolour, sculpture, digital art, printmaking and fabric art. The juror is Jesse Firestone, Associate Curator of the RAY Triennial of Photographic Arts in Frankfurt and Curator of Exhibitions at The Interchurch Center in New York, who has organised more than 60 exhibitions and works on artist agency and experimental approaches to exhibition-making. First place takes a $300 award and two further artists receive honourable mentions. The call’s own timeline opens applications on 15 October 2026, closes them on 15 November, juries 16–19 November, notifies artists on 20 November and runs the online exhibition from 20 November to 31 December 2026. Entry is $30 for one to three images, all work must be original and artists must be 18 or over. Every piece must be for sale for the run of the show at a price fixed at application and cannot be withdrawn early; the artist keeps 60% of any sale, and submitting grants the gallery promotional use of the images.
