The River We Call Home

Prize Exhibition

A free, curated two-museum group exhibition marking the 75th anniversary of the Connecticut River Conservancy, organised by the Brattleboro Museum & Art Center with the Conservancy and the Connecticut River Museum. The brief is about a watershed as a lived-in place rather than scenery: the exhibition "honors the Connecticut River watershed as a place of wonder, belonging, and relationship-one that sustains both human and natural communities", and asks artists to reflect "a legacy of love and stewardship through artistic interpretations of the river as a home". Suggested entry points include the river "as a place of beauty, reflection, belonging, or identity", "Inhabitants of the watershed, great or small", "Relationships between people, wildlife, and water", "Change over time-ecological, cultural, or personal", stewardship and "Daily life, recreation, or quiet moments along the river". Readings may be "literal or abstract, historical or modern, celebratory or contemplative". Photography is named in the accepted media: "All media will be considered, including but not limited to: Painting, drawing, printmaking, Photography and digital media, Sculpture and installation, Fiber, textile, and mixed media, Assemblage or found-object work." THE GATE IS GEOGRAPHIC, NOT A RESIDENCY RULE: the call is open to artists who "Live, work, or have a meaningful connection to the Connecticut River watershed (in New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts, or Connecticut)" and who can deliver or ship work to either institution; artists at all career stages are encouraged, and collaborative and interdisciplinary work is welcome. Selected work hangs in two linked shows at riverside venues: the Connecticut River Museum in Essex, Connecticut, February 13 to May 30, 2027, with an opening on March 25, and the Brattleboro Museum & Art Center in Brattleboro, Vermont, July 10 to October 31, 2027, with an opening on July 10. Artists may state a venue preference but "The curators will determine in which venue selected work will be displayed." Up to three works may be entered; the official Wufoo entry form carries exactly three work blocks, each with one required image and one optional detail image, plus a 250-word artist statement about your connection to the watershed and a 100-word bio. A curatorial panel reviews every submission, no jurors are named, and notification is due by December 4, 2026. There is no entry fee on any surface. There is also no promised award: BMAC says only that "Sales information, honoraria, or insurance details (if applicable) will be provided upon acceptance", so treat an honorarium as possible rather than published, and note that "Artists are responsible for delivery and retrieval of artwork unless otherwise noted" and that selected work "must be delivered ready to hang/display". BMAC itself is an independent, non-collecting contemporary art museum near the confluence of the Connecticut and West Rivers; it runs a standing Patrick and Marcelle Leahy Fund for Photography, and the Connecticut River Museum is accredited by the American Alliance of Museums.
