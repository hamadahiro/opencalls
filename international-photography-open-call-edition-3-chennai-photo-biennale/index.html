International Photography Open Call — Edition 3

The CPB Foundation, which runs the Chennai Photo Biennale, opened the third edition of its International Photography Open Call on World Photography Day 2026. It is open to anyone, from anywhere, on any subject, with no age limit, though applicants under 18 need guardian consent. Entries can be single images or a photo series of ten to twenty pictures, and there is no cap on how many entries one photographer may make. The foundation asks for a cohesive body of work rather than a set of unrelated pictures under the series category, and for a concept note or captions with each submission; English is explicitly not a judging criterion. Entries are reviewed by an international jury of curators, institutional directors and industry figures, still to be announced for this edition, and past panels have included Alessia Glaviano of PhotoVogue, Bharat Sikka, Julie Héraut of LE BAL, Gwen Lee of DECK and Michael Weir of Belfast Photo Festival. Selected finalists are printed and exhibited free of charge to them in a large-scale public exhibition spread across Chennai, with the possibility of a travelling show in another Indian city and digital showcases at partner festivals. Two Sigma 35mm f1.4 DG DN Art lenses go to one single-image and one series winner. Entry is priced in rupees on the foundation own store, tiered by where you live: ₹885 for a single image and ₹1,660 for a series from outside South Asia, ₹300 and ₹700 for residents of India and South Asia, and ₹100 and ₹200 for students in India. Each extra work is charged on its own tier — ₹100 per additional entry for students in India, ₹300 for residents of India and South Asia and ₹820 from outside South Asia, in variants of one to four, so a four-work international series entry reaches ₹4,940. Notifications are due in late December 2026. PAYING FROM OUTSIDE INDIA IS A TWO-STEP DETOUR: the open call's own International Payments page warns that "some international payment methods [are] not working on our Payment gateway" and routes non-Indian applicants to fixed PayPal links in euros — EUR 8, 15, 16, 23, 24, 30, 32, 38, 40 or 45 — chosen to match the rupee cart total, after which a discount code zeroes the cart at checkout; South Asian residents outside India get a parallel rupee ladder of INR 300 to INR 2,100.
