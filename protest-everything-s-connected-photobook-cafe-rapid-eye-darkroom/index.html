Protest, Everything's Connected

Prizes Exhibition Zine publication Mentoring Street gallery display

This is the eighth of Photobook Cafe's quarterly open calls, and the theme is the one subject photography has never been able to leave alone. The organisers are blunt about not wanting the obvious version of it: protest is "visible in the streets, in our homes, online, in landscapes, within communities and in the everyday choices we make", and what they say they particularly welcome is work that moves beyond documenting a protest as an event — the people behind the movement, the spaces they occupy, what activism leaves behind, the quiet refusals nobody photographs. If you have a march set, that is eligible. If you have the thing after the march, that is what they asked for.

The range named in the call is unusually specific for a themed group show: climate and environmental action, social justice and equality, anti-war and peace movements, Indigenous rights, feminist, LGBTQIA+ and disability rights activism, "or any other movement advocating for change". Method is open too — documentary, conceptual, portraiture, landscape, still life, archival material, experimental image-making, analogue or digital, new work or personal archive, made anywhere in the world.

The judges are working photographers rather than institutional names. Rebecca Zephyr Thomas has photographed LGBTQ+ activists for Dazed and Ukrainian protesters for Elephant and worked with the Green Party and Extinction Rebellion; Jade Smith is a director, photographer and filmmaker whose practice centres on protest, community and social documentary. They choose 35 photographers for the show and zine launch at The Photobook UK Gallery on 11 November, and announce Best in Show from the floor that evening at 19:30.

What the 35 actually get is layered, and the layers are the reason to enter. Ten receive a 30-minute one-to-one mentoring session on portfolio development, project direction and professional practice. Three Best in Show photographers are carried into a Photobook UK Public Programme group exhibition in 2027, with printing support from Rapid Eye Darkrooms. Ten have work installed in an UNCLE street gallery across London — the same images, out of the room and into the city. Exhibition prints are produced by Rapid Eye Darkrooms on digital C-Type paper, and the organisers state there are no additional costs to exhibit if you are selected. And all 35 keep something physical: the press release says each finalist receives a complimentary copy of the group zine featuring all 35 selected photographers, "along with an exhibition print of their selected image(s)".

£25 buys one to ten images, and you can enter as many times as you like at that rate. Entering is two separate steps and the form will not let you skip one: pay the £25 on the organiser's Squarespace store first, then upload your images to the Google Form together with a screenshot of the payment receipt, which is a required upload slot of its own. The spec is JPG, 2400px on the long edge, 300dpi, RGB, named forename_surname_photograph title_No., and if you are selected you may be asked for a higher-resolution file. The organisers call the open call not-for-profit and say where the money goes: a portion to Not A Phase and Just Stop Oil, picked by the judges, the rest into the free photobook collection, the gallery programme and youth engagement. Eligibility is as wide as it gets — emerging, established or amateur, any age, background and identity, anywhere in the world. One practical caveat, and it is the gallery's own: the space has no step-free access and is reached by 25 steps.
