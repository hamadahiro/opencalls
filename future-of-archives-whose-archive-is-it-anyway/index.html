Future of Archives: Whose Archive Is It Anyway?

Prize £200 speaker fee

Open call for speakers for Whose Archive is it Anyway?, a one-day event at The Photographers' Gallery on Friday 16 April 2027 and the first in a three-part series exploring the future of archives and archiving. The brief asks what makes an archive and who decides: who creates one, what it consists of, who it is for, who has access, and what becomes of the everyday material that sits outside institutional collections, hidden, forgotten or displaced. The Gallery is explicit that it wants submissions of real-life experience rather than academic papers, and the call is aimed particularly at people working outside traditional institutions and archives. Suggested subjects include artists' and photographers' legacies, reclaimed photographic archives, family archives, counter-archives that question dominant historical narratives, grassroots and radical archives, questions of ownership and authority over images and their stories, memory and lost histories made visible again, digitisation and digital preservation, and technologies that reduce barriers to archives while safeguarding material. Selected speakers receive a fee of £200. Applicants must be 18 or over and available to attend the Gallery in person on 16 April 2027. There is no application fee. Applying takes a short Microsoft form: name, email, a talk title (which can be a work in progress) and a 400-word summary, plus confirmation that you can present in person on the day. Applications close 1 November 2026 at 11.59pm BST and selected speakers are notified by 18 January 2027. Supported by TownsWeb Archiving.
