Future of Archives: Whose Archive Is It Anyway?

Open call for speakers for Whose Archive is it Anyway?, a one-day event at The Photographers' Gallery on Friday 16 April 2027 and the first in a three-part series exploring the future of archives and archiving. The brief asks what makes an archive and who decides: who creates one, what it consists of, who it is for, who has access, and what becomes of the everyday material that sits outside institutional collections, hidden, forgotten or displaced. The Gallery is explicit that it wants submissions of real-life experience rather than academic papers, and the call is aimed particularly at people working outside traditional institutions and archives. Suggested subjects include artists' and photographers' legacies, reclaimed photographic archives, family archives, counter-archives that question dominant historical narratives, grassroots and radical archives, questions of ownership and authority over images and their stories, memory and lost histories made visible again, digitisation and digital preservation, and technologies that reduce barriers to archives while safeguarding material. Selected speakers receive a fee of £200. The call is open to all, and the Gallery has dropped the in-person requirement it carried in August: the event is now described as “a hybrid event (online and in person audience and speakers) so welcome international submissions too”, and the only footnoted condition left is “You must be 18 years or older to speak at this event.” There is no application fee. Applying takes a short Microsoft form: name, email, a talk title (which can be a work in progress) and a 400-word summary, plus a question about availability on 16 April 2027. That form was re-read on 29 October 2026 and it settles the point: the availability question now reads “Are you available to give your talk in person at The Photographers’ Gallery on Friday 16th April 2027? (the event will be hybrid)” with Yes and No both allowed, and the form’s own header says “This event is hybrid so international speakers or those who cannot attend in person can speak online.” Applications close 1 November 2026 at 11.59pm BST and selected speakers are notified by 18 January 2027. Supported by TownsWeb Archiving.
