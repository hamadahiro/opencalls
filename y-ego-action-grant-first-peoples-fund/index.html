Yéego Action Grant

Prize $100–$2,500

First Peoples Fund's Yéego Action Grant exists for the moment a working artist needs money quickly and there is no cycle to wait for. It offers $100 to $2,500 to individual Native artists and culture bearers who need "financial assistance with a specific professional opportunity or towards a hardship that is hindering their creative practice." The name carries the Diné word *yéego*, a phrase of encouragement that can mean "keep going" or "don't give up." The eligible uses are concrete and unusually practical: entry or workshop fees to take up an art opportunity; travel, lodging and meals to attend a residency, seminar or similar professional development; expenses from an emergency such as fire, theft or a natural disaster; repair of damaged equipment or tools; unanticipated transport needs, from a vehicle repair to a bus pass; unexpected medical or health costs that stop you working; and sudden unforeseen expenses on a current project, such as replacing damaged supplies or finding a new venue. What it will not do is equally clear — no reimbursement for money already spent, no general purchases of new tools, materials or equipment, no tuition or student loans, no organisational operating support, and no funding for new or existing projects, exhibitions or programming. Applicants must be at least 18, must be enrolled members of or provide proof of lineal descent from a US federally or state-recognised tribe, or proof of Alaska Native or Native Hawaiian ancestry, must be practising artists, must not be enrolled in a degree-seeking programme, and must not have received a Yéego grant before; current FPF grantees, fellows and leaders of grantees are ineligible, and group or multiple-artist requests are not considered. The Submittable application adds one bar the programme page omits: an applicant "must be a U.S. based resident". There is no fee. Applications run through Submittable and ask for three recent examples of your work with a brief description of each, used to confirm you are a practising artist; a website or social link is optional and explicitly does not affect scoring, because FPF "recognise[s] that not everyone maintains an online presence." Supporting links and up to two documents — an invoice, a quote, a budget — are also optional. The grant runs on a monthly rhythm: the portal normally opens by the 12th, the deadline is the 10th of every month at 3:00 p.m. Mountain Time, applicants hear by the last day of the month, and approved funds arrive roughly six to eight weeks after the monthly due date. The round open at the time of writing closes Saturday 10 October 2026. FPF staff and leadership review the applications. One thing to know before applying: the fund is open to artists of every discipline and First Peoples Fund's own pages enumerate no list of accepted media, so nothing here is specific to photography — but nothing excludes it either, and camera repair and market entry fees are named uses.
