Sustainable Darkroom Futures Residency

The Sustainable Darkroom is seeking five UK-based artists to propose research and development projects for innovative, sustainable systems in photography. Each resident receives a £3,400 grant across two instalments, bi-weekly online seminars with speakers including Renata Marques Leitão, Rob Hopkins, Tim Crossland and Dominic Wilcox, bi-weekly online mentorship with project-specific development, and access to facilities and technical support at the University of Sunderland for testing and prototyping. The six-month hybrid programme culminates in an exhibition and symposium in Sunderland in October 2027, with a travel stipend and accommodation, plus inclusion in an online publication of the final research documents. The organisers ask applicants to “think beyond the established norms in this sector” and to envision “radical, unorthodox, even outlandish ideas” about photographic systems — material, philosophical, political, legal or structural — and say outright they are not seeking submissions on already well-established processes such as plant-based developers or botanical cyanotype toning. Prototypes, blueprints, test images and written papers all count as outcomes. Two of the five places are reserved for residents of Northern England (South Yorkshire to the Scottish Borders) and two for artists with disabilities; applicants may fulfil both criteria. Audio and video applications up to 10 minutes are welcome. Run in partnership with the Northern Centre of Photography and the University of Sunderland research group Bridging Photographies, Cultures, Communities, and supported by Arts Council England and The Grocers’ Charity. Online Q&A sessions on 23 September and 11 October. Free to enter; open to UK residents only. Shortlisted applicants are interviewed 2–9 December 2026.
