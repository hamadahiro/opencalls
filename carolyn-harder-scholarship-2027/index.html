Carolyn Harder Scholarship 2027

Prizes $1,000 Solo exhibition Photography Atelier place

The Griffin Museum of Photography in Winchester, Massachusetts runs a set of annual scholarships and prizes alongside its exhibition programme. The Carolyn Harder Scholarship is the newest of them, created to honour Carolyn G. (Grande) Harder (1943-2024), an art history graduate of Elmira College who taught young students and spent years as a docent at the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.

The award is $1,000, and the money is the smaller part of it. The recipient gets a solo exhibition at the museum in the Fall and a place in Photography Atelier - the Griffin's year-long project and portfolio development programme - which is the kind of sustained mentorship an emerging photographer rarely gets attached to a prize.

Eligibility is deliberately early-career: students, recent graduates and early-career photographers, worldwide, with no age limit and no residency requirement. Photographers currently enrolled in a photography degree programme may apply. The museum states plainly that the scholarship is not for well-established photographers, and names two hard disqualifiers - receiving a Fulbright Scholarship, and having a photo book published by a publisher. Self-publishing does not disqualify. Museum staff, board members and jurors' immediate families are excluded.

Entry is free. Applications run through CaFE: the form accepts 5 to 10 images, a single PDF carrying both a short biography and an artistic CV (the museum asks that the PDF filename include "Harder" plus your last and first name), a statement of artistic purpose, and a statement on the work submitted.

The application period for the 2027 cycle opens Monday October 5, 2026 and closes Friday November 6, 2026 at 23:59 Mountain Time, and the museum says it will announce the awardee by January 1, 2027. Emily Belz and Jennifer McClure are named as the jurors of the cycle Cassidy Thurber won; the 2027 CaFE listing reproduces the same two biographies without attaching them to a cycle, and the museum has not stated who juries 2027.
