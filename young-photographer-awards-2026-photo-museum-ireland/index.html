Young Photographer Awards 2026

Photo Museum Ireland, the national centre for contemporary photography, runs the Young Photographer Awards as its national photography awards for young people, and the 2026 edition is open to anyone aged 8 to 18 living on the island of Ireland. The museum's own terms scope it to young people resident in the Republic of Ireland or Northern Ireland who were aged between 8 and 18 inclusive on 31 December 2025 — born between 1 January 2007 and 31 December 2017 — and entries are judged separately in two age groups, 8–13 and 14–18. Entry is free, each entrant may submit up to ten photographs, and the museum is explicit that no previous experience or specialist equipment is needed: photographs made with a phone, a digital camera, an analogue camera or another photographic process are all accepted. Judging is on three equally weighted criteria — strength of the idea, creativity and originality, and visual impact — with the photographer's age taken into account; judges see the photographs, titles and age group only, not names or contact details. One overall Young Photographer of the Year is selected from the six age-category winners, with three Rising Stars winners drawn from the 8–13 group, three from the 14–18 group, and a wider shortlist of Highly Commended photographers. Winning and shortlisted photographs go into a large-scale public outdoor exhibition on Admiral Browne Walk at Sir John Rogerson's Quay in Grand Canal Dock, Dublin, presented with support from IPUT and launching in late November 2026. Entrants under 16 need a parent or guardian to authorise the entry through a secure email link before it reaches the judges, and an unauthorised entry is deleted after seven days. Images containing elements produced or modified with artificial intelligence must be identified and categorised as such at entry, and entrants must not present AI-generated material as wholly their own photographic work. Submissions open on Monday 31 August 2026 and close at 11pm on Sunday 11 October 2026, Irish time, through the museum's CameraComp entry portal.
