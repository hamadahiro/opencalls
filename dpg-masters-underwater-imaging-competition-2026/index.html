DPG Masters Underwater Imaging Competition 2026

This is the one underwater photographers plan their year around. The organisers' own phrase for it — "the 'World Championship' of international underwater imagery events" — is marketing, but the jury is not: Nicolas Remy chairs, and the panel includes David Doubilet, who has shot more than 75 stories for National Geographic since 1971, alongside Jennifer Hayes, Henley Spiers, Ipah Uid Lynn and Renee Capozzola. The prize pool is over $90,000 and is paid in dive trips with resorts and liveaboards and in imaging and dive gear rather than cash, which is the currency this field actually runs on.

Eleven categories, and they are drawn with unusual care about what kind of photographer each one protects. "Traditional" allows only global adjustments of brightness, contrast, colour and sharpness — no cropping, no cloning, not even backscatter spot removal — explicitly so that "people who are not as savvy at photo editing" compete on even ground. "Unrestricted" goes the other way and permits composites, painted backgrounds and unlimited particle removal. Smartphone and Compact Camera are fenced off from interchangeable-lens cameras. Cold Water wants work shot below 18°C, Blackwater wants pelagic macro on a black background, Portfolio wants exactly six images, and Short Film wants five minutes or less with at least half the footage underwater.

The AI rule is worth reading before you edit: AI-powered upscaling, de-noising and sharpening are allowed, but adding elements that were not in the original capture — marine life, parts of animals, divers, torch glow — "including with the use of generative AI, remains forbidden, and will be verified by reviewing RAW files." Keep your RAWs.

Entry is $10 per image or video, with 15% of entry proceeds donated to marine conservation. Entering one image in three categories counts as three entries, and the Portfolio category is still a single file — six photos laid out on a neutral grey background, 6,000 pixels on the long side. Selected finalists are also offered a mentor prize — an hour-long one-to-one video session with one of the judges to review their portfolio — which for an emerging underwater photographer may be worth more than the gear. Submissions close Friday 8 January 2027 at 11:59PM PST, and the rules print the whole calendar after that: shortlisting 11–15 January, judging 16–28 January, RAW files from finalists by 31 January, awards decided 1–3 February, winners announced late February.
