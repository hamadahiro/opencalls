1839 Awards Art of Photography Contest

Prizes $1,500 1st prize 1839 Magazine interview

The 2026 edition of the 1839 Awards' Art of Photography Contest, which the organiser frames as celebrating "the creativity, vision, and distinctive perspectives that make photography an art form, across every genre". It takes over from the Art of Storytelling Contest, whose own page is still up but carries the finished 2025 edition (8 September 2025 to 14 January 2026); the site now lists three annual contests: Photographer of the Year, Art of Photography and Color Photography. MIND THE TWO DEADLINES. The contest page's headline reads "Deadline: November 18", but its own submissions-due panel sets out three stages: early bird 26 October only, regular entry 26 October to 18 November, and an extended entry deadline running 18 November to 18 December. Entries are therefore still accepted until 18 December, which is the date carried here — but the prices below are the regular-stage ones, and the contest's own pricing record discounts the regular stage while leaving the extended stage undiscounted, so expect to pay more after 18 November. Pay-per-entry pricing runs in three tiers — $15 student / $25 non-pro / $30 pro for a single image, and $30 / $40 / $50 for a series on the same tiers — with credit bundles at $88 / $105 for five and $175 / $210 for ten, which the organiser advertises as up to 30% cheaper than paying per entry. A professional is defined as someone who sells or publishes regularly or has earned the majority of their income from photography; everyone else may enter as a non-professional. There is also a rolling fee-waiver route: 1839 runs a scholarship programme for applicants from communities underserved or underrepresented in the creative world, applied for by email with a short statement naming the contest. Awards are cash and the figures are published: $1,500, $749 and $250 for the three professional places and $900, $400 and $200 for the three non-professional ones, $3,999 in all, which this contest's page rounds up to "$4,000+ in cash prizes". The eighteen category placings (gold, silver and bronze in each) carry a trophy and a certificate but no cash. There is also international media coverage, a profile in the 1839 Artist Directory, a published interview in 1839 Magazine, and press releases, badges and certificates for awarded work. Judging is blind and two-stage, with a nominated shortlist per category and aggregated judge rankings deciding the winners; the three judges shown on this contest's page are drawn from a wider 1839 panel of nineteen that includes staff from the Whitney Museum, Phillips, Christie's, Getty Images, the Centre Pompidou, Bonhams, Serpentine, Vanity Fair and the New York Times. Entry is open worldwide to anyone eighteen or over, excluding staff and family of parent company Creative Resource Collective, LLC and of the jury. Previously published, exhibited or awarded images are welcome, there is no requirement that the work be recent, and exclusivity is not required. Files must be JPEG or PNG, at least 1500 pixels on the longest edge and under 10MB, unwatermarked; film, digital and phone captures are all accepted in any orientation. You keep all rights — "we do not become the owner of your work when you submit an entry", no copyright or moral rights are claimed and entries are not sold on, though 1839 may use submissions to promote the competition with credit. ON AI THE ORGANISER CONTRADICTS ITSELF, AND FOR THIS CONTEST THE ANSWER IS NO. Its central rules page says, twice, that "AI-generated images are only admissible in a designated category as specified in any 1839 Awards contest", while this contest page's own entry-eligibility panel carries the flatter line that "AI-generated images are not admissible in any 1839 Awards contest". The contest's own category list settles which of the two applies here: eighteen categories, from Abstract and Documentary to Street & Urban Life and Wildlife, and not one of them is an AI category, so there is nowhere in this call for an AI-generated image to be admitted. 1839 also reserves the right to demand proof that an image was not AI-generated.
