Benton Fall 2026

Prize Exhibition

A juried, themeless call for the Salem Foyer on the main floor of the Benton Convention Center, part of a curated fine-art series the Benton runs in partnership with Associated Artists of Winston-Salem, the artists' association founded in the city in 1956. AAWS is blunt about what the venue is and who walks through it: "Hosting countless events, conferences, and meetings from C-Suite and corporate businesspeople, the Benton Convention Center is a great venue for exposure." There is no required theme. The distinguishing feature is scale — the foyer is "well-suited for very large 2D art", and the call explicitly frames itself as "an excellent opportunity for very large 2D work which may not always be practical and allowed in other exhibits", with limits of 10 feet in height and 8 feet in width and a 50-pound ceiling per piece. Photography is affirmatively named in the accepted media: "All 2-dimensional fine art media accepted for selection", and separately, "Hand-pulled prints, original digital prints and photographs are all acceptable"; printed reproductions of paintings and drawings are not exhibited. The show is genuinely selective rather than first-come — members may submit up to six works for consideration, no more than four will be taken from any one artist, and roughly 15 to 25 pieces in total are chosen from digital images by a committee of AAWS jurors, who are not named. Work previously shown at the Benton is ineligible, though work entered and not selected in an earlier round may be re-entered. Entry is $20 for up to six works and is members-only: AAWS membership is bought online at $75 a year, or $20 for high-school students over 15 and college students, and is not itself juried — the general level lists "Entry into all exhibitions, including highly-renowned juried shows". Two constraints a photographer should weigh. First, the venue imposes content rules: "No nudes, and nothing controversial, i.e., nothing political religious, or potentially offensive to members of the general public." Second, there is no cash award of any kind — what comes back is the hang, from October 26, 2026 through the fall into mid or late January 2027. Entries close October 12, 2026, with acceptances sent no later than October 16 and drop-off and installation in person on October 26 between 9am and noon; pick-up is mid to late January, date to be confirmed. Sale prices are not displayed in the foyer — an AAWS plaque directs enquiries to the association — and artists set their own prices, receiving 70% with AAWS retaining a 30% commission plus 7% North Carolina sales tax. Accepted images are retained for publicity during the show and may be archived and used on the AAWS website.
