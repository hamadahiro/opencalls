Ronan Guillou Grant #4

Prizes €4,000 2-month residency Exhibition

The Ronan Guillou Grant is the most concrete thing in the Eyes Wide Open programme: €4,000, a place to stay, and a date in a museum. One professional photographer from Europe or from a country bordering the Mediterranean is picked to spend two months around Sète, the Étang de Thau and the Gulf of Lion, making an engaged project about people and the marine world - ecology, human activity, heritage and transmission, economic and geopolitical pressure, or the region's relations with the rest of the Mediterranean basin. The work is shown in September 2027 at the Musée de l'Étang de Thau in Bouzigues, inside the second FLOW Biennale, which runs 18 September to 31 October 2027.

The money breaks down in the regulations: "a grant of €4,000 (including €3,000 in remuneration and €1,000 in per diem)". On top of that the organisers cover two return journeys to Sète and your accommodation during the residency. Everything else - production, prints, kit, any travel beyond those two journeys - is yours. You get five months to finish, of which up to two months are on site, and the residency can be split into two stays between February and June 2027 around your other commitments. The call page, the regulations and the French application form all agree on the five months; one sentence in the form's English block says "a two-month period to develop their project", which is the on-site figure, not the project window.

The gate is narrow in one way and wide in another. Narrow: you must be a professional, and duos cannot apply - the regulations say so outright. Wide: the two documents disagree on the geography and the wider one is the one you sign - the regulations, Article 4, say the call "is open to European photographer candidates or photographers from a country bordering the Mediterranean", while the call page and both language blocks of the form say "the southern shore(s) of the Mediterranean". If you are on the northern or eastern Mediterranean rim and outside Europe, the binding text admits you and the marketing copy does not. There is no age limit, you can be independent or represented by an agency, collective or gallery, and the programme is "open to all forms of photographic creation", with a stated preference for original documentary, post-documentary and fine-art approaches. Archives and video are explicitly inside the frame - the grant supports "an artistic project using images (photographs, videos, archives, etc.)". An eco-responsible approach, in the subject, the production technique or the way you propose to show the work, is an assessment criterion rather than a requirement.

Applying is two moves and people will get caught by the second. First, the online application form - identity, residence, project title, a short description, and what support you would need for the residency. Second, a single PDF sent by SwissTransfer to the association, named SURNAME_FIRSTNAME_PROJECT TITLE, with the subject line "RGG #4_LAST NAME+FIRST NAME". That PDF carries your biography and CV, a project statement of 3,000 to 6,000 characters (with illustrations or a mood board if the project is already under way, and any collaborating authors, scientists or organisations named), a portfolio of up to 25 MB, and the regulations with your signature on them. The SwissTransfer link has to be under 50 MB and valid for a week. French or English both accepted.

The cutoff is not midnight. The dossier has to be in by 6 p.m. Paris time on Sunday 15 November 2026 - the regulations say "no later than 15 November 2026 at 6 p.m. (Paris Time)" and the form repeats it. The jury sits at the end of November, the winner is contacted privately and then announced in December 2026, and the residency begins in February 2027 at the earliest. No juror is named anywhere: the regulations describe "a jury composed of the BRG organisers and experts in photography and/or the marine world".

Read the rights clause before you write the statement. "The grant recipient assigns their copyright to the organisers until 31 December 2027 for uses that may be made of the selected 25 images exclusively within the framework of the Ronan Guillou Grants and the FLOW programme of which it forms part: exhibition, catalogue, communications, internet and social media." An assignment is heavier than a licence, but this one has both an end date and a purpose scope, and it bites only on the 25 images you deliver. What you owe at the end of the residency is those 25 photographs with captions and a presentation text; sketches, a notebook, other multimedia or sound material are welcome but not required.

The grant carries the name of Ronan Guillou, a photographer from Roscoff and, in the association's words, "a humanist and passionate observer of the marine world", who died suddenly in 2022. The fourth edition is supported by Sète Agglopôle Méditerranée, M28-Terres de culture, the Musée ethnographique de l'Étang de Thau, Hahnemühle and the Tomoé laboratory. Juliette-Andrea Élie won the 2025 edition with "La Grande Nacre".
