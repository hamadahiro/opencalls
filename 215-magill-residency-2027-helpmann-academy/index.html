215 Magill Residency 2027

Prizes 12-month studio Fringe exhibition

Helpmann Academy's 215 Magill Residency gives one emerging South Australian visual artist or designer a dedicated studio for twelve months at 215 Magill, a design studio and workshop in Maylands, Adelaide, where a community of practising artists and makers works in furniture, art and design. The residency is valued at A$6,000 in kind rather than paid as a fee: an allocated studio space from 1 February 2027 to 31 January 2028, 24-hour access with security and WIFI, access to and training on a full machine shop for woodwork, use of a ceramics kiln, and mentoring from 215 Magill founder Mark Jones. It ends in a group exhibition with the other artists-in-residence at the fab workshop and studios, held at St Peters Town Hall Gallery as part of Adelaide Fringe in February–March 2028. Photographers are inside this one even though the workshop's own tools lean towards timber and clay: Photography is one of the artform specialisations the application form itself offers under Visual Arts, alongside painting, sculpture, ceramics, installation, moving image, print making and new media. What narrows the door is not medium but geography and career stage. Applications are invited from graduates with a practice in visual arts holding a supported degree from Adelaide University, Flinders University, the University of South Australia or the University of Adelaide, up to seven years from graduation. Honours students and graduates qualify where honours followed directly on from the undergraduate degree; Masters and PhD graduates and candidates are not eligible unless they still qualify through an undergraduate or honours degree. Anyone with an overdue acquittal on a previous Helpmann award is ineligible. Two Helpmann surfaces disagree on how long the door stays open: this residency's own Information for Applicants page says seven years from graduation, while the academy's general Who We Support page allows five years measured from 31 December of the final year of study and lists only two other programmes as seven-year exceptions. The eligible degrees are closed dropdown lists on the form, so anyone near the edge of either window, or whose course name is not listed, should ask the academy to confirm eligibility before writing — both pages invite exactly that. Applying is free, but the required material takes time to assemble: three written responses of up to 500 words each, an artist CV of no more than two pages, an official academic transcript showing the conferral date, a letter of support on letterhead from a current or former lecturer or from an industry professional who can speak to the practice — uploaded with the form, never sent separately — and up to five images and/or audio-visual demos, each file no larger than 10MB and no more than 50MB in total. Reviews or press coverage of past work, extra support letters and further audio-visual material are optional additions. Applications opened at noon ACDT on Tuesday 13 October 2026 and close at 11:59 pm ACDT on Tuesday 24 November 2026. An optional studio tour on Wednesday 28 October 2026 lets applicants meet 2026 resident Chelsie Morey and Mark Jones and see the machinery and spaces before they write, and Helpmann's programme manager is available to discuss an application online or in person before submission. An unnamed panel assesses every application on artistic merit, the impact of the residency on practice and career growth, the vision for the equipment and the creative community, and readiness to take it on; shortlisted applicants may be interviewed and the panel's decisions are final. The recipient commits to at least 20 hours a week in the studio, holds public liability insurance for the duration, supplies images and content for Helpmann's social channels, and credits the academy in publicity for the work it supports.
