Mono Orbit Awards: EMISSION

Prizes €2,100 in cash awards Publication in a printed photobook and digital catalogue

The first edition of a new international monochrome photography award devoted to the built environment, open to entries until 6 January 2027. The theme, EMISSION, asks for the visible and invisible traces left by the environments we create — energy, pollution, light, heat, waste, movement, production, density — read either literally or conceptually, and the brief explicitly reaches past architecture to infrastructure, industry, construction, transport, energy systems, public space and human-shaped landscapes. Documentary, conceptual and experimental approaches are all invited, and the organiser asks that monochrome contribute to a photograph's structure, atmosphere or meaning rather than function as an effect. There are no categories and no public voting: every eligible photograph goes through one selection process, from which twelve photographers are chosen as THE ORBIT 12 — three take the principal awards of €900, €700 and €500, and nine are recognised as Selected. The €2,100 cash fund is guaranteed once at least twelve different eligible photographers have entered; if that minimum is not reached the edition does not proceed to selection and all entry fees are refunded in full. All twelve selected photographers are published in a printed photobook produced through Blurb and in a digital catalogue, with editorial presentation and a place in a continuing archive — the organiser states plainly that complimentary printed copies are not included and that a paid printed edition needs the photographers' separate permission. Award recipients additionally receive individual editorial interviews, and are invited to sit on the jury of the following edition. Honourable Mentions may be given at the juror's discretion. The first edition is judged by a single juror, POSTRH, who is also the award's founder. Entry is €20 for one to four photographs, open worldwide to photographers aged 18 and over; files are JPG in sRGB, maximum 3000 pixels on the longest side and 4MB each, with no watermarks, logos, borders or identifying text. Generative AI must not be used to create or substantially replace photographic content, conventional post-production including tonal adjustment, cropping, retouching and monochrome conversion is permitted, and RAW files, source files, film scans or metadata may be requested to verify photographic origin. The organiser also states it does not intentionally use submitted photographs to train AI models. Cash awards are paid within 30 calendar days of the announcement of results; no results date is published.
