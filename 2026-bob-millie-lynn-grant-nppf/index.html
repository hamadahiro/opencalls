2026 Bob & Millie Lynn Grant

A $5,000 grant from the National Press Photographers Foundation for a long-term documentary project photographed in the United States — work that takes a creative approach to important stories that might otherwise go untold, with the emphasis on ethical, narrative-driven journalism and on underreported or overlooked aspects of American society. It is named for Bob Lynn, who was assistant managing editor for graphics at The Virginian-Pilot and its photojournalism leader for 17 years, wrote Vision, Courage & Heart, and received the NPPA's Joseph Sprague Memorial Award. Judges weigh the strength and impact of the storytelling alongside image sequencing, technical quality and adherence to professional photojournalism standards; no juror is named. Both students and working professionals may apply, provided they are legally authorized to work in the United States, Puerto Rico or the US Minor Outlying Islands, and the funded work must be photographed in the US. You submit up to 24 images or three video stories, a project proposal, a budget and a short resume; entries can be edited up to the deadline, ongoing and collaborative projects are eligible, and existing publication deals must be disclosed. Two funding bars are published, on two different surfaces: the grant page excludes anyone who has received more than $10,000 in other funding in the past three years, and the application form excludes anyone who has had more than $5,000 in grant funding in the three years before the deadline. Staff photographers need written permission from their employer first. AI imagery is barred outright and using it to make images or video disqualifies the entry. There is no entry fee. Payment comes in two parts: $4,000 on award, then $1,000 on delivery of at least 25 captioned high-resolution images within 18 months, with a written account of the completed work due in 12 to 18 months and monthly check-ins with an NPPF coordinator throughout. Recent recipients include Bob Miller, Ed Kashi, Laura Morton, Caitlin O'Hara, Kathy Shorr, Mary Calvert and Nathan Posner. Applications run through Picter, whose counter reads 1 December 2026 at 04:59 UTC - that is 11:59pm Eastern on 30 November, the close NPPF's own page states. The open reading is the one with the working submission route behind it; do not take the chip for a closure.
