Life Framer — Solitude

The December round of Life Framer's twelve-part monthly photography award, judged by Johan Lolos, the Belgian–Greek landscape photographer known for sweeping, emotionally resonant images of the earth's wild places — Alpine peaks, the Gobi desert, the colour-infused Icelandic highlands, the jagged Greek coast. The brief asks for solitude in all its forms: the quiet clarity of a single person, the vastness of an empty landscape, the eloquence of negative space, chosen or imposed, expansive or intimate. Life Framer names a lone person in a crowd, an isolated animal in the wild, empty streets at dawn, minimalist scenes stripped to their essentials and objects set apart from their surroundings, running from contemplative portraits and sparse still life to sweeping vistas and subtle details. All genres and approaches are welcome — documentary, conceptual, candid, staged, studio, environmental — with new work or images from the archive. The award is open worldwide to amateur and professional photographers aged 18 and over; the organisers accept all types of photography from analog and digital devices including mobile phones and allow editing, post-processing, mixed media, collage and compositing provided photography is at the heart of the work, but state that they do not accept artificial intelligence or AI-generated imagery. Entry is priced by the number of images: $20 for one, $30 for three, $40 for six, or a $100 membership covering up to ten images in every monthly themed competition for twelve rolling months. The monthly first prize is $2,000, drawn from a $24,000 annual fund shared between the twelve monthly first-prize photographers, and the first- and second-prize images each month are exhibited in galleries around the world. Photographers retain full copyright, and Life Framer states it does not sell usage rights or use entries to train AI algorithms.
