Fixed Focus Fellowship 2027

Prizes $1,000 credit Group exhibition

Year-long fellowship at Aurora PhotoCenter, the artist-run photography centre in Indianapolis, for up to 10 Indiana photographers aged 18 to 26 — with no art-school enrolment required and no limitation on subject, style, working method or academic training. The 2027 cycle runs 29 January to 31 December 2027 and gives each fellow a free one-year Aurora membership with darkroom, scanner and printer access; free places on Aurora's core workshops (developing black-and-white film, developing colour film, making a black-and-white print, scanning basics, digital printing basics), each offered monthly for the length of the fellowship; a $1,000 use-it-or-lose-it credit for darkroom rental, scanning, digital printing and alternative-process workshops charged at the member rate; one-to-one mentoring arranged with founding director Mary Goodwin; professional sessions on pricing work and applying for grants and exhibitions, worked on real applications; a Final Friday feedback dinner with the cohort every month; and a group exhibition of work made in the programme opening 3 December 2027, with all work for sale. Aurora frames it for photographers outside Indianapolis with limited access to facilities, recent graduates who have lost campus darkrooms, self-taught photographers documenting family and community, multidisciplinary artists strengthening the photographic side of their practice, studio and fashion photographers moving toward fine art, and anyone wanting to work seriously in cyanotype, wet plate collodion, platinum-palladium, van dyke, gum printing or photograms. Applicants who work primarily in another medium may apply with a project that incorporates photography. Fellows buy their own film, paper and materials. Applications open 15 August 2026 and are submitted by email: up to 10 images or supporting files, a statement of interest of up to 250 words, a short biography of up to 200 words including your age, and two references. Selection is by Mary Goodwin with a group of local arts leaders.
