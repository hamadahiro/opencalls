Inspired Annapolis

Visit Annapolis and Anne Arundel County has given Maryland Federation of Art a show at the Annapolis Visitor's Center at 26 West Street in Historic Annapolis, a recently relaid space that around 800 people walk through every week, and MFA is filling it from this call. Entries must be inspired by or representative of Annapolis, Anne Arundel County or the 250th anniversary of the United States. Two-dimensional work only, in any medium, with no dimension exceeding 34 inches including edges and frame; photographic prints must have been analogue or digitally processed by the artist, who must have controlled cropping, colour balance, contrast and the final printing, and any limited-edition photographic print must be signed and numbered. Work made with image-generative AI is not accepted, and no reproductions are. All MFA exhibitions are blind juried, with artists' names and identifying information hidden from the juror; this one is selected by Jo Fleming, artist and director of Jo Fleming Contemporary Art, who took a BFA at MICA, grew up in the Appalachian mountains of Virginia and has shown abstract paintings about coal mining and global warming at the US Embassy in Ulaanbaatar and the American Institute in Taipei under the State Department's Art in Embassies exchange. Selected work hangs at the Visitor's Center from 2 November to 31 December 2026, with an in-person reception on 4 November; every entry meeting the criteria is considered. The juror's Best in Show award is a $70 one-year MFA membership. Entry is restricted to MFA member artists residing in any state or territory of the United States, Canada or Mexico, so a non-member must buy a membership — currently $70 a year — before entering; the entry fee itself is $15 for one or two works plus $5 for a third, and up to three original works made in the past three years may be entered. Artists are notified by 5 PM on 19 October and keep 70 per cent of sales, 75 per cent for members. Founded in 1963, MFA is a non-profit whose Circle Gallery in Annapolis is Maryland's oldest operating artist gallery. MFA retains a 30 percent commission on any work sold through it (25 percent for MFA members), so artists keep 70 or 75 percent of a sale.
