2027 Artist in Residence

Prize 2-3 week residency with park lodging or campsite

Great Basin is one of the least-visited national parks in the lower 48, and that is the point of this residency. Eastern Nevada, five hours from anywhere, a 13,063-foot peak, a cave system, bristlecone pines, and some of the darkest skies in the United States. The park has run an artist-in-residence programme with the Great Basin National Park Foundation since 2007, and it is free to apply. Three places are awarded from this call, all taking place during 2027, and applicants say which seasons they want to be considered for.

The three residencies are not equivalent and the differences are practical. Winter is two to three weeks in January or February with park housing — a furnished private bedroom in a shared living space, kitchen, bathroom, laundry. Most roads are closed and the park is likely under several feet of snow; snowshoes or cross-country skis are how you get around. Summer (July or August) is a camping residency, with a full set of camping gear supplied by the Foundation but no electricity at the campsite unless you bring your own RV, no internet at campgrounds, showers in Baker. The fall term (September or October) can be timed to overlap the park Astronomy Festival in September — but check what you are signing up for, because the two surfaces contradict each other on it: the park's own page lists fall as a housed residency on the same terms as winter, and says fall and winter residencies with NPS housing require a background check, while the CaFÉ prospectus calls fall a camping-only residence. Be honest with yourself about the remoteness before applying: the nearest town has few or no amenities and Ely is 60 to 70 miles away.

The call is open to artists residing anywhere, with one of the three places held for a regional artist living and working in Nevada or Utah. The park media list names visual, literary, musical and other creative-media artists without enumerating photography, and the programme overview page lists past selections as painters, illustrators, musicians, composers, poets, architects, sculptors, carpenters and metal workers — but the park own roster of previous artists settles it: Jack Straton (photography, fall 2025), Paul Atkinson (astrophotography, fall 2024), Ken Koenig and Lisa Rose (photography, 2012) and Deon and Trish Reynolds (photography, 2007), and a current resident who works in film photography. Note the obligations: residents become NPS volunteers, present one public programme in the park, deliver a virtual programme within six months and a digital portfolio within three, and donate one physical work plus a digital representation to the Foundation under a deed of gift, which may be displayed in the park for up to three years. One caution on the deadline — the park page heading says apply September 15 to October 30 and the CaFE listing agrees, while a paragraph lower down the same page still says September 1 to October 15; the two current surfaces point to 30 October.
