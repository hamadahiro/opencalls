The Aftermath Project 2026 Post-Conflict Grant

Prize $25,000

A single $25,000 production grant, free to apply for, from the project Sara Terry founded out of her own five-year body of work on the aftermath of the war in Bosnia: photography that tells “the other half of the story of conflict — the story of what it takes for individuals to learn to live again, to rebuild destroyed lives and homes, to restore civil societies, to address the lingering wounds of war while struggling to create new avenues for peace”. One winner is funded and four finalists are named. The brief is deliberately wide on geography and scale and narrow on subject: the conflict may be an international war, a rebel insurgency, an urban riot or violence between rural ethnic groups, and the Project says it generally looks for stories “no longer being covered by the mainstream media, or which have been ignored”; proposals about the aftermath of domestic violence or environmental disaster are turned away unless clearly framed as the aftermath of conflict. The 2026 letter, signed by interim executive director Andrew Cullen, notes that several recent recipients have worked on conflicts in Ukraine and Gaza that are still running, and that the grant exists so that we “should not look away”. This is a production grant for still photography — explicitly not a distribution grant for exhibitions or books, and grant money may not be spent on video or audio even where a project includes them. An application is a signed form, a two-page proposal opening with a one-sentence summary, a portfolio of up to 30 jpegs (1200 px on the longest side, 72 dpi, under 2 MB each, named LastName_1.jpg), a caption sheet and a two-paragraph bio, sent by WeTransfer, Drive or Dropbox to the Project's email; there is no budget requirement, and it is fine not to have started the project. Open to working photographers worldwide; full-time students are not eligible. Photographers keep all copyright. The winner is paid in three parts — half at onset, 40% at the interim report, the last 10% on delivery of twelve 16x20-inch or larger prints for the Project's archive — with the work due by January 1, 2028 and prints by May 1, 2028; the winner then makes at least 30 images, and finalists 10–15, available for possible exhibition, publication and the Project's lesson plans, with any press-use fee split 50/50 with the photographer. Applications close at midnight PST on December 1, 2026, with the winner and finalists announced by the end of that month.
