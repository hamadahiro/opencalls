International Mute Photo Festival 2027 — Nature Ate the Pixels

Photonisos Arts & Culture, the Samos non-profit behind the East End – Samos Arts Festival, is launching the first International Mute Photo Festival with a single, uncompromising rule: every photograph must be captured and printed on a children's instant thermal camera — the cheap toy that spits low-resolution black-and-white images onto thermal paper. The festival frames this as a deliberate subversion, taking Guy Debord's warning about a world replaced by its own images and answering it with a medium too coarse to flatter anything. The theme, Nature Ate the Pixels, asks for work in which human presence sits inside the natural world rather than above it, split across four categories: Landscape (nature with no human trace), Symbiosis (the body meeting water, earth and flora), Rebirth (nature reclaiming the cityscape) and Future (children discovering the natural world). Post-processing is almost entirely forbidden — you may crop the printed timestamp and white borders and convert to black and white, and nothing else; the lines, marks and noise of the thermal print are treated as the work's core identity, not as faults. Entrants submit high-resolution scans of their original prints, and the jury may ask to see the physical strips to verify the medium. Fifteen adult series and five from the under-18 category are selected, printed and enlarged entirely at the festival's expense, published in a printed catalogue, and shown in July 2027 at the renovated Old Customs Warehouses at Karlovasi Port on Samos. The artist behind the most complete series gets travel and accommodation covered for the opening. Entry is free, and artists retain full copyright. Submissions open 1 September 2026 and close 30 April 2027 at 23:59 EEST; adults send 6 to 9 images with a 250-word statement by email as a single ZIP, and under-18 entries of 5 images must come from a parent or guardian's address with a written consent declaration in the body of the email.
