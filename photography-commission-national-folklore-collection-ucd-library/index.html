Photography Commission: National Folklore Collection

Prizes €20,000 commission Exhibition

A €20,000 photographic commission from UCD Library, marking the move of Ireland's National Folklore Collection out of the Newman Building — its home since 1971 — and into the new Cultural Heritage Centre inside the James Joyce Library. The NFC is internationally recognised as one of Europe's most important ethnographic archives, and its Irish Folklore Commission Collection (1935–1970) has been inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register since 2017. One photographer or lens-based artist will make an original series of 15 to 25 works responding to the transition, with facilitated access to the archive spaces, the reading room, selected collection material, and the librarians, archivists, researchers and conservators who work there. The brief is deliberately open to documentary, conceptual, research-led and contemporary approaches, and suggests themes from archives and memory to conservation, institutional architecture, and the relationship between place and memory; the panel asks for proposals that are evocative, optimistic, dynamic, democratic and human-centred, framing the relocation as renewal rather than an ending. A selection of the finished work goes into a public exhibition, anticipated for May 2027, with printing, framing and installation commissioned separately by the Library. Copyright stays with the artist, who grants UCD Library a perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to exhibit, publish and archive the work; commercial licensing needs a separate agreement. At least 25% of the proposed budget must be allocated to the artist's fee, and the commission is paid in four quarters against contract, research, draft and final delivery. Applications are a single PDF of up to 20MB — CV, artist statement, a 500-word commission proposal, a 500-word research methodology, an indicative timeline, an itemised budget and five portfolio images — emailed by 12 noon. Site visits run 31 August and 1 September, bookable by email, and the deadline for queries is 4 September. UCD Library says it particularly welcomes applications from artists underrepresented in the arts and cultural sector, and offers accessibility supports and alternative submission formats on request.
