The 9th John Ruskin Prize — Not for Present Delight

Founded by the Guild of St George in 2012 and managed by Parker Harris under the administrative umbrella of The Big Draw, the John Ruskin Prize is a multidisciplinary art prize open to artists, designers and makers worldwide, amateur or professional, aged 18 and over. The ninth edition takes its theme, Not for Present Delight, from The Seven Lamps of Architecture, where Ruskin urged makers to build for their descendants rather than the present moment: the call asks what it means to create with an awareness of consequence, while also posing the challenge of whether art must serve a purpose beyond itself. Photography is named explicitly among the accepted media, alongside "Photographic Elements, Including Those Manipulated or Collaged" and "Collage, in all materials, including photography"; the prize has honoured photographers in past editions. Work must have been made in the past four years and submissions are judged digitally, with installations, diptychs and triptychs counted as a single artwork. The selection panel shortlists up to 80 artists for an exhibition at Trinity Buoy Wharf in London, running 28 January to 21 February 2027 with an awards evening on 27 January. Four winners share £9,500: a £3,000 first prize from the Guild of St George, the £1,000 Alan Davidson Under 26 Prize, the £3,000 Ruskin Mill Trust Prize for a beautifully crafted functional object, and the £2,500 Chelsea Arts Club Trust Prize for a mid-career artist without gallery representation. A GreatArt People's Choice Award adds £500 of materials. International entrants pay a reduced fee and are not obliged to ship work, showing online and in the catalogue instead. Entries close at 5pm UK time on 17 November 2026, not end of day. Entrants confirm at submission that the work was created by a human.
