Darkroom Residencies 2027

Three six-week darkroom residencies at Spike Print Studio in Bristol for artists working in alternative photographic processes, running January to June 2027. Spike Print Studio is a charitable organisation and, in its own words, "the largest open-access print studio in the South West", printmaking in Bristol since 1976. This call is open only to artists based in the South West of England: the studio states it is "looking for three South West based artists who want to research, experiment and expand their current practice". Each residency gives up to four days of darkroom access a week (Mondays and Tuesdays guaranteed, Wednesdays and Thursdays bookable), a £200 materials budget, a place in a three-person exhibition at Spike Print Studio in June 2027 - timed to coincide with Spike Island's launch of British Art Show 10 - and a paid artist talk at Spike Island, hosted by Spike Print Studio, at which the three residents will appear in conversation together. Named areas of technical enquiry are cyanotype, gum bichromate, lith printing, phytogram, lumen printing, cliché verre and pinhole camera, and the studio is explicit that the list is not exhaustive. The darkroom holds a Durst M300 and a Durst C65 black-and-white enlarger (both 35mm), a printing frame, focus finder, test strip maker, contrast filters, a 1.2 x 1 m Natgraph metal-halide UV exposure unit, a table-top etching press with blankets and a 60 x 30 cm bed, an A2 lightbox, trays and tongs. Applicants must be able to work confidently and independently in their field, must come with a particular project or research enquiry to pursue over the six weeks, and must be willing to lead a small workshop for Spike Print Studio members. The three residency windows are 18 January to 4 March 2027 (no access in the week of 15 January), 8 March to 22 April 2027 (no access in the week of the 29th) and 26 April to 8 June 2027 (no access on 3 and 31 May), with the exhibition and artists' talk in June 2027, date to be confirmed. Applications opened on Thursday 5 November 2026 and close on Sunday 29 November 2026 at 23:59 GMT; shortlisted applicants are interviewed in the weeks of 30 November and 7 December 2026, and the selected artists are notified in the week of 14 December 2026. The application is a Google Form, and it asks for more than the call page lists: a 500-word statement on why you are applying and how the residency will benefit your practice, a 300-word description of your areas of expertise and the alternative photographic techniques you use, your preferred residency window, between three and five images of your work each under 1 MB, a referee's name, email and organisation, and your age. A CV may be attached but is optional, and a Google account is needed to upload the files. There is no application fee.
