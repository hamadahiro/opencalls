SÍM Residency — January–February 2027

Prize Residency

Open call for one- to three-month residency stays beginning in January or February 2027 at the SÍM Residency in Reykjavík, run by SÍM – the Association of Icelandic Visual Artists. The programme describes itself as “one of the Nordic region's most established artist residency programmes, welcoming around 200 artists and cultural practitioners from around the world each year”, and it offers private and shared studios, accommodation, an orientation meeting, an artist page on the residency's own website and social channels, and opportunities for public presentations and artist talks. It is open to “all creators and art practitioners” working in “all mediums of visual arts, design, architecture, music, dance, performance art, creative writing, curation, cultural work, and scientific research”; photographers are among the residents the programme publishes, including projects built by “repeatedly assembling, photographing, dismantling, and reassembling” found Icelandic materials and a multidisciplinary body of work made “through painting, photography, and printmaking”. THE ARTIST PAYS: SÍM is a nonprofit, self-funded programme with only partial facility support from the City of Reykjavík, so applying is free but a selected artist owes a 50,000 ISK non-refundable programme fee within 15 days of acceptance plus a monthly accommodation fee of 140,000 ISK for a single bedroom-studio and 210,000-280,000 ISK for a twin - the first month payable two months before arrival, also non-refundable. BUDGET THE HIGHER FIGURE: the page prints its room prices twice and the two tables disagree on two of the twins - the application form's own room selector charges 240,000 ISK for bedroom 18 and for bedroom-studio 2, where the Residency Fees block lists them at 210,000 and 230,000 - so one month in the cheapest room costs at least 190,000 ISK all in. The fee covers administration, a SÍM membership card, wi-fi, parking, bedding and towels, communal kitchen equipment, laundry, heating, water and electricity and basic tools in the project space; meals, materials, insurance, travel, living costs and workshop fees are not included, and the fee “does not constitute rent” so a resident does not become a tenant. Applicants must hold at least a BA, though “exceptions may be made for self-taught artists who demonstrate high professionalism in their artistic practices, supported by a compelling CV and project proposal”. The application asks for a 400-word project description covering the conceptual framework, research questions and working methods, three images of previous work (10MB total) and a CV; proposals touching sustainability, ecology, global warming, geology, the environment, Icelandic culture or Nordic language are flagged as an advantage. January and February applications are reviewed as they arrive with a reply within two weeks, and the residency notes a vibrant shared-living environment with communal kitchens that may not suit everyone.
