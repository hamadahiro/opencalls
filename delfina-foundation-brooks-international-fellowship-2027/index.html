Delfina Foundation — Brooks International Fellowship 2027

Prizes Funded fellowship £40/day living allowance

Two six-month fellowships run jointly by Delfina Foundation and Tate, now in the programme's thirteenth year, placing international researchers inside Tate Britain's Curatorial Division from 5 April to 17 September 2027 while living in residence at Delfina Foundation in London. The two strands are separate calls with separate application forms. The first, Leonora Carrington: Art and the Theatre of Life, asks a Fellow to undertake foundational research into the British-Mexican artist's interest in theatre, performance and performativity — locating, analysing and contextualising theatrical materials across archives, manuscripts, correspondence, photographs and ephemera and situating them within Carrington's transnational career; Tate notes that a Fellow with knowledge of the Mexican social-political context and of performance histories in the second half of the twentieth century would be well placed. The second, Artist Supported Studios, contributes to research and development for the 2028 Tate Britain Commission through the lens of artists who work with supported studios — organisations that enable disabled and neurodivergent artists to sustain professional practices through long-term studio provision, advocacy and creative support — examining how collaborative artistic practices can be documented and interpreted while remaining attentive to artistic agency. The position is non-salaried, and full time: 36 hours a week for six months, broadly four days at Tate to one at Delfina Foundation. Fellows receive a research resource allowance, travel to Tate Modern, Tate Liverpool and Tate St Ives and to other UK and European institutions, a return economy flight, UK airport transfers, reimbursement of visa fees with support through the visa process, a £40 per day living allowance, a London travel pass, and a single bedroom at the Delfina house, which hosts up to eight residents around shared bathrooms, laundry, kitchen, library and dining room. Fellows get access to Tate's collection, archive, staff and networks, and opportunities to present their own practice at Tate and Delfina events. Open to international researchers based outside the UK. Applications are not made online: the relevant Word application form is downloaded from the Tate listing and emailed to Tate, with an alternative video or audio format available on request. The deadline is Wednesday 21 October 2026 at 17:00 GMT; shortlisted candidates are contacted by 30 October, two rounds of Zoom interviews run between 5 and 27 November, and successful candidates are notified by 4 December 2026. Tate asks that the words in the application be the applicant's own, warning that responses which read as generic or artificially generated could count against an application or see it rejected.
