Eighth Annual Online Juried Exhibition

Prizes $250 $150 $100

The Idaherma Museum of Art Foundation, a small Princeton-based 501(c)(3) set up to find and promote artists worldwide, runs an annual online juried show with real money behind it: the David Latin Williams Foundation award of $250, second prize $150, third $100 and two honourable mentions of $50 each, all posted out to the winners. Every accepted entrant gets at least one work shown, not only the prizewinners — the foundation publishes each edition as a browsable online exhibition captioned by medium. This year's juror is Elizabeth Massa, a New Jersey photographer, printmaker and teaching artist who holds a graduate certificate in documentary photography from the Salt Institute and studied street photography in Venice; her statement asks for work with a distinct point of view — how you interact with the materials of your medium, how you see the world around you, how your work reflects the objects of daily life, travel, the natural world or your dreams — and says plainly that the show is open to all visual media. The foundation's eligibility line still asks for original works on paper, but its own exhibitions have never been held to that: ten of the 120 works in the fourth annual show are photographs, oil and acrylic on canvas are throughout, and a photograph took second prize in the seventh. Work must be original and not made in any class or with any instructor within the last three years. Nothing is mailed: work is judged and displayed from files, sized between 5 x 8 and 24 x 36 inches, with the long edge no more than 1500 pixels, at least 72 dpi, unwatermarked, unframed, in JPG or PNG. Open to artists 18 and over. AI-generated work is not accepted, and the foundation reserves the right to display submitted work for one year.
