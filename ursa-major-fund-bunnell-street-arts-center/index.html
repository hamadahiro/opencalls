Ursa Major Fund

Prize 10 × $6,000

The Ursa Major Fund is Alaska's Andy Warhol Foundation Regional Regranting programme, administered by Bunnell Street Arts Center in Homer. It makes ten grants of $6,000 each to Alaska artists and artist collectives for artist-led, risk-taking, public-facing projects that inspire curiosity, engagement, dialogue and respect for cultural integrity. Funded projects should take place in Alaska, be led by Alaska artists and be intended for Alaska audiences, must be artist-organised and artist-initiated, must include a public-facing component that is free and open to participants, and should be completed within the calendar year of the award. Projects that form part of a nonprofit's or a business's own programming, that are part of an MFA or BFA course, that lack a free public component, or that take place at Bunnell Street Arts Center itself are not funded. Eligibility runs to artists and culture bearers over 18 who define Alaska as home; artists with an LLC may apply, 501(c)(3)s and for-profit businesses such as galleries may not, and MFA or BFA students may collaborate but not lead. A valid social security number is needed to receive payment. The application window opens on 1 September and closes on 15 October each year, with office hours available throughout and statewide Zoom information sessions in August and September. A jury drawn primarily from outside Alaska adjudicates on artistic excellence, community impact and feasibility during November and December; grantees are notified on 31 December, sign agreements by 15 January, receive 80 per cent of the award by 31 January and the balance on filing a project summary the following December. The application is a Submittable eligibility form plus an application form with work samples attached and a budget on the fund's own template. Entry is free, through Submittable. The first cohort, announced for 2026, funded work ranging from a mobile art trailer touring Alaskan communities to an online glacier archive, a Tlingit spruce-root weaving exhibition and arts programming for villages served by the Selawik National Wildlife Refuge.
