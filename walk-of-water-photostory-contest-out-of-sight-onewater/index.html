Walk of Water Photostory Contest: Out of Sight

Prize US$9,000 total

Onewater's Walk of Water Photostory Contest returns for a sixth edition, and this one goes looking for what nobody commissions. "Out of Sight" spans the full breadth of water and health — how water shapes health, longevity and well-being — and aims its main prize squarely at wastewater: dignified toilets, clean rivers, and the damage untreated sewage causes to people and ecosystems.

The organisers are unusually specific about the work they think is missing. They want wastewater surveillance made visible: sampling at treatment plants, drains, hospitals, airports and transport hubs; laboratory analysis; data interpretation; the public-health response that follows. Stories on surveillance are asked to connect the scientific work to the communities, decisions and actions it supports — to show how wastewater reveals pathogens, antimicrobial resistance and other emerging threats without testing every individual.

The theme is wider than the pipe, though, and the call says so plainly. The river that cools a city, the ritual bath, the swimming lesson, the tap that finally runs safe, the mental-health and spiritual value of blue spaces, sport in pools and lakes and on community pitches, and safe drinking water and sanitation across homes, schools, workplaces and care facilities are all Out of Sight territory, and all of it can be selected, exhibited and published.

A submission is a photo story, not a single frame: 5 to 10 photographs (JPG, at least 10 megapixels, long side 3,872 px or more), a written narrative, numbered captions, and a signed photo licence — co-signed by a parent or guardian for entrants under 18. The page states the narrative length twice and differently — 2,500–8,000 characters in one copy of the How to enter block (with 30–300 character captions), 100–400 words in the other — so write to whichever is stricter for your story until the organisers reconcile them. Up to three stories per participant, and no participant takes more than one prize. Basic adjustments are permitted; adding or removing picture content is not, and AI-generated images are not accepted.

Read the licence before you commit the work. Submitting places your photographs and your story under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0). That is more open than the promotional licence most calls ask for: you keep your copyright and remain free to sell and license the pictures elsewhere, and every reuse has to credit you and carry the same licence onward — but the grant is public and non-exclusive, so the organisers, their partners and, as the terms put it, "anyone else" may share and adapt the work, commercially included. The portal's photo licence agreement states the work "can and will be published and reused" by Onewater and its partners under it. For an EU-funded, UN-convened project on public health that openness is the point; it is still a decision to make with your eyes open, because a Creative Commons grant cannot be withdrawn later.

The US$9,000 pool runs in two tracks. The Global Wastewater Award carries US$6,900 — prizes of $3,000, $2,000 and $1,000 plus three $300 merit awards, each with a feature on the Onewater website. The Youth Wastewater Award carries US$2,100 for photographers aged 25 or under on 1 December 2026, judged across the whole theme rather than wastewater alone.

Money is not the whole of it: the strongest stories of the edition, wastewater or not, go into the 2027 Walk of Water exhibitions and are published with Onewater's media partners worldwide. Past editions have hung at UNESCO HQ in Paris and travelled to New York, Almaty, Delft, Lake Constance and Vancouver.

The jury is drawn from photography, water science and the UN system, and includes conservation photographer and SeaLegacy co-founder Cristina Mittermeier, photographer Pablo Tosco, Agency Perspective CEO Fabeha Monir, UNEP communications director Daniel Cooney and Michela Miletto, coordinator of the UNESCO World Water Assessment Programme. Entries are scored on visual storytelling, originality, photographic quality, writing and relevance to the theme; the decision is final.

Entries open 1 October 2026 and close 30 November 2026 at 23:59 CET, through Onewater's own contest portal. Winners are announced in January 2027.
