Visual Artist AIRspace Residency 2027–2028

Abrons Arts Center, the arts programme of Henry Street Settlement on New York's Lower East Side, supports a cohort of three early-career visual artists across a 20-month residency that begins January 5, 2027 and runs to August 2028 — the Submittable prospectus defines Year 1 as January to August 2027 and Year 2 as September 2027 to August 2028, with the closing group exhibition opening in spring 2028. Each resident gets a semi-private studio at Abrons for the full 20 months, an annual honorarium of $7,000, an annual materials stipend of $5,000, 30 hours of fully subsidised time in the centre's dance studios, studio visits with curators and arts professionals, professional development with the Arts Education department, paid opportunities to share work in progress through public programming, and a group exhibition at Abrons at the end of the residency. Every resident is paired with a professional artist mentor who is an alum of the programme, meeting one to one each month; the 2027–2028 mentors are Park McArthur (AIRspace 2013–14), Baseera Khan (2016–17) and Elliott Jerome Brown Jr. (2019–20), a Brooklyn-based artist and photographer whose own practice uses photography to explore representation through privacy and fiction. The call is for visual artists and names no medium list, but photographers are visibly part of the programme's lineage through Brown and past fellows. Applicants must be based in New York City and living within the five boroughs both at the time of application and during the residency, must not be in a degree-granting programme or a student during the residency period, and must fit Abrons' own definition of early career — actively making and sharing original work but without many opportunities yet to exhibit, take up residencies or grow professionally, with age and years of practice explicitly not factors. Applications are free, made through Submittable, and close October 4, 2026 at 11:59pm ET; the residency page adds that the call also closes early if 200 applications are reached. Review runs to November 2, in-person interviews are November 16–20, and invitations go out in December 2026. Funded by the Jacques and Natasha Gelman Foundation.
