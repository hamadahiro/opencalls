Prime Desert Woodland Preserve Artist in Residence Program

Prizes $1,500 stipend Residency Exhibition

This is a museum residency rather than a prize, and the thing being judged is a direction of inquiry, not a finished body of work. MOAH asks explicitly for "a general direction of inquiry rather than a predetermined finished project" and says the residency is intended to let artists respond to what they encounter at the Preserve over time. A photographer who arrives with a locked-down shooting plan is answering a different call.

Photography is named in the prospectus twice — once among the disciplines the programme supports and once among the forms the final exhibition may take — so a lens-based applicant is not reading themselves into a general art call. The work samples asked of visual artists are five images labelled with name, title, medium, dimensions and year.

The timetable is tight at the back end and the prospectus says so plainly: the residency ends 23 May 2027 and the exhibition opens 5 June 2027, so "artists should not expect the period following the residency to serve as the primary production period for the exhibition." Work, research and exhibition materials have to be developed during the 12 weeks.

The geography is the hard gate. Ten named Southern California counties, 8–12 documented visits to a site in the Antelope Valley, and a hotel allowance the prospectus prints twice and inconsistently — "Up to six hotel stays" in the Residency Overview list, "up to eight hotel stays" under Accommodation and Working Conditions — so an applicant from Imperial or San Diego County is eligible but should plan travel against the lower figure until MOAH settles it. The $3 CaFÉ fee is the only cost of applying.
