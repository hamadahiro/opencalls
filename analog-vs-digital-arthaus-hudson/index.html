Analog vs Digital

Quarterly group show at the ArtHaus Gallery, part of ArtHaus Hudson — a Weehawken, New Jersey arts organisation at 4700 Park Avenue that also runs creative workshops, after-school and music programmes. This edition, opening January 2027, is about the meeting of hand and machine: the organisers want “works that contrast, blend, or investigate the conceptual tension between tactile, handmade processes and modern, pixel-based, or computer-generated practices.” Photography is named in the eligible media alongside painting, drawing, printmaking, digital art, illustration, collage and mixed media, which makes this one of the few calls where an analogue print and a digitally made image are the point rather than a category problem. Two things to weigh before applying: the show is deliberately small — a maximum of ten artists are accepted — and sculpture and freestanding three-dimensional work are excluded outright, so every submission must be wall-mountable and arrive ready to hang with wire or D-rings (unframed canvases need clean or painted edges). All work must be for sale; the gallery retains a 30% commission, so the artist receives 70% of the retail price. The application is a short form on the gallery's own page — bio, medium and links to the work — and the $15 non-refundable fee is paid separately by Zelle with the applicant's name in the payment notes.
