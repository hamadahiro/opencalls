Candace Freeland Photography of Merit Award 2026

Prize $4,000 in cash awards

A free, cash-funded photography award for young photographers, founded by the photojournalist Candace Freeland (1952-2021) — an Associated Press, Charlotte Observer, New York Times and Washington Post contributor whose exhibitions included Forced Out: The Agony of the Refugee in Our Time for Amnesty International — and now run by The Mitford Museum in Hudson, North Carolina, the museum founded by her mother, the novelist Jan Karon. US residents aged 8 to 22 are invited to submit photographs made on a cell phone on the theme Food and Family. There are two categories, colour and black and white, judged in two age groups, 8-15 and 16-22; you may enter one photograph in each category provided the two images are markedly different, and entering both still leaves you eligible for only one juried award. Files must be high resolution and able to resize to an 11 by 14 inch print. The jurors are Maggie Flanigan, director of the AFA Visual Arts programme at Caldwell Community College, whose own lens-based work looks at religion and community in the rural South; Tim Barnwell, an Asheville photographer with work in the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Asheville Art Museum; and Tom Rankin, who heads Duke University's MFA in Experimental and Documentary Arts. Each of the four category-and-age-group slots carries a $500 Grand Prize, a $250 Runner-Up and a $250 People's Choice voted by the public, and the museum runs free smartphone photography workshops in the run-up. Winners are announced at a reception on 9 October 2026. Note that the museum's two surfaces disagree: its Merit Awards page gives a 1 November deadline and a $750 top prize for the 16-22 group, while the official rules attached to the entry form give 1 September, $500 across all four slots, and the October reception. The form is the document entrants agree to, so enter by 1 September.
