Environmental Photography Award 2027

Prizes €5,000 €1,000 × 5 categories €500 × 2 audience awards Exhibition Publication

The Prince Albert II of Monaco Foundation's annual award for photographers working in the service of environmental awareness, now in its seventh year. Created in 2021, the prize sets out to celebrate the beauty of the planet and to show the different relationships humans hold with nature — destructive and regenerative alike — across five categories: Polar Regions (landscapes and wildlife beyond the Arctic Circle, in Antarctica or the sub-Antarctic islands, shown as wild space free of human presence); Forests (the flora and fauna of boreal, temperate and tropical forest ecosystems); Ocean (marine and ocean ecosystems, underwater images only — river and lake images are not accepted in this category); Changemakers (the positive reality of human-nature relations, the efforts and initiatives of individuals and organisations building a world more respectful of nature); and Humanity versus Nature (the negative reality — human-wildlife conflict, the destructive impact of human activity, drought, fire, extreme weather, biodiversity loss, poaching and the depletion of global water resources; extremely violent or gory images are not accepted). An international jury of professional photographers selects 36 finalist photographs and names the category winners and the grand prize winner. The grand prize, Environmental Photographer 2027, carries a €5,000 grant and an invitation to the exhibition opening in Monaco with travel and accommodation included; each of the five category winners receives €1,000, with two runners-up named per category; a Public Award and a Students' Choice Award — the latter voted by high-school students of the Principality of Monaco — each carry €500. All 36 finalists are shown in the Monaco exhibition and on its tour, which has previously travelled to Italy, San Marino, Spain, the United States, Canada and France, and are published in the bilingual French-English hardcover award book, of which they receive two copies. Entry is free and open worldwide to professional and amateur photographers aged 18 or over on 1 November 2026; employees and agents of the Foundation and of Photocrowd are not eligible. Photographers may enter up to five images in each of the five categories, for a maximum of 25 in total — entering more than five in a category invalidates all of that photographer's entries. Images must have been shot within the past five years (no earlier than 1 September 2021), at a minimum of 3000 pixels on the shortest side, saved as highest-quality JPEGs at 300 dpi, preferably with an Adobe RGB (1998) profile, free of watermarks, text, copyright symbols, logos and frames, and carrying their original camera metadata. A title and supporting information — especially the species shown and the exact location — are essential; images submitted without them are unlikely to be considered. Previously awarded images are eligible. The Foundation sets explicit welfare conditions: subjects must have been treated respectfully before, during and after the shoot, images of captive animals are not allowed except where the image documents a specific situation that is clearly described in the caption, and live baiting is not permitted. Images must not deceive the viewer or misrepresent the reality of nature; moderate adjustment to levels, curves, colour balance, saturation, dodging, burning and dust-spot removal is acceptable. AI image generation is strictly prohibited, including the addition of AI-generated elements to a camera original and generative-fill extension beyond the original frame — all longlisted images are verified against their RAW files. The shortlist forming the exhibition and book is announced on 1 April 2027, when the two-week public vote opens; the grand prize, category, Public Award and Students' Choice winners are announced on 26 April 2027, and the book follows in June 2027. Images not selected for the final shortlist are not used by the Foundation in any way. The award is one of three pillars of the Foundation's Green Shift initiative promoting inspiring ecological narratives, and Sergio Pitamitz is the competition's president. Entries are submitted on Photocrowd, which requires a free account.
