Prototyping Futures of Making: Water, Data, and Living Systems

WEAD — Women Eco Artists Dialog, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit created in Northern California in 1996 by Jo Hanson, Susan Leibovitz Steinman and Estelle Akamine and still run by a volunteer board — is taking entries for its 2027 member exhibition of ecoart and social justice art. The theme turns the data centre back on itself: at a moment when, as the call puts it, "AI/Data Infrastructures depend on vast quantities of water and land, this exhibition asks what it means to build within systems that are already alive." It wants practices that engage water, data and living systems as sites of construction and consequence — trees transmitting through root and fungal networks, rivers carrying memory and contamination, natural systems registering signals that exceed human language, set against computational systems that abstract, store and intensify those flows. The framing draws on material ecology and interdisciplinary design, naming Neri Oxman's work and Paola Antonelli's curatorial perspective at MoMA, and looks for work that moves past fabrication toward growth, co-production and interspecies collaboration. The jurors say they will prioritise work that closely addresses issues and solutions on this topic. All media are accepted, including sound and video, so photographic and lens-based work is eligible on its own terms. Two jurors: Lisa B. Woods, whose practice moves across art, technology, ecological systems and interdisciplinary research, and Leah Dalton, curator of the physical exhibition at The 109 Gallery in Chickamauga, Georgia. The show runs 1 May to 31 August 2027, online and in the gallery, and comes with a printed catalog, artist presentations on Zoom, a panel discussion, and awards — the organisers say awards will be given but have not published their values. Entry itself is free — one entry per artist, of one to three pieces — but this is a membership show: WEAD states that you must be a paid member to be eligible to be juried, with payment due by the 31 January deadline. The call names a $45 annual fee; WEAD's own membership page prices the basic level at $45 a year and a youth, student and low-income level at $20, says limited scholarships are available on request, and lists the chance to apply to the annual membership shows as a benefit of every level. All women-identified, non-binary and trans-identified artists are invited to join and apply. Works sold through the exhibition carry a 25% commission to WEAD. Entrants pick either online-only or physical-and-online, and anyone taking the physical option pays their own shipping both ways, prepaid return included, plus their own insurance. Work shown in a previous WEAD exhibition is not accepted. The cutoff has a time: the EntryThingy call block dates it "Entry deadline Jan 31, 2027 - 11:59 PM".
