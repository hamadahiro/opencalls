200 Years of Photography: Images, Representation and AI

Prize Exhibition at the Goethe-Gallery

An open call to join a week of intensive photography workshops in Hong Kong marking two centuries since the first lasting photographic image, and asking what a photograph means when anyone can fabricate one. The Goethe-Institut Hongkong and the French Consulate of Hong Kong run the project with the School of Creative Media at City University of Hong Kong, supported by the Franco-German Cultural Fund. Successful applicants are assigned to one of three workshops: Gwenola Wagon (France) on image-making in the age of AI, from anarchives to speculative narratives; Andreas Müller-Pohle (Germany) on Machine Images, developing concepts from idea to finished work; and a third on photojournalism in the age of AI, covering fact-checking techniques for identifying AI-generated images and how visual truth is being redefined. The format is two virtual preparation sessions with your mentor in September or October, then three full days in person in Hong Kong on 9–11 October. Work produced during the workshops — experiments, sketches, short films and ideas in progress — goes on public display at the Goethe-Gallery in Wan Chai, opening the evening of 12 October, followed by a public symposium at City University on 13 October. Applications are welcome from students in photography or journalism, early-career practitioners and young professionals working with images, and anyone with a strong interest and some experience in photography, AI and visual culture. Applicants must be 18 or over, apply individually, and commit to the preparation sessions, the full three-day workshop and the exhibition opening.
