Anything but…Plastic

Prize Exhibition

Riverside Arts Center, a 501(c)(3) arts centre on Huron Street in Ypsilanti, programmes Anything but…Plastic into its 2026–27 gallery season as a group exhibition built around a single material constraint. The call opens with the dictionary definition of plastic and then throws it out: no acrylic paint or gesso, no synthetic fibres, no found objects containing plastic, no Mod Podge or plastic adhesives, no 3D printing, no plastic or acrylic beads. 'Acrylic paint? Get outta here! Try some homemade egg tempera! Nylon thread? No way!' The centre's allowed list is oil paint, watercolour, natural fibres, wood, charcoal, oil pastels, ceramics and glass, and it is explicitly non-exhaustive — 'include, but are not limited to' — with an invitation to get in touch if you are unsure about your medium. Photography is not named in either list, so read the constraint as the brief rather than as a bar: a resin-coated print is polyethylene-coated and will not clear it, and an inkjet paper with a polymer receiving layer is exactly the case the gallery asks you to raise with it, while fibre-based silver gelatin, cyanotype, platinum-palladium, salted paper and other plastic-free processes plainly can. The ban is scoped to the work itself — the prospectus adds that frames and installation materials are not included — and the gallery's concurrent exhibition Deconstruction is described in its own words as combining photography, drawing, painting and found-object art. Entry is free — the centre states on its Artist Opportunities page that all of its calls for art are always free to enter — and the call is open to artists of all ages, locally, regionally and nationally, with priority possibly given to artists based in south-eastern Michigan. Applications go through Artwork Archive, which caps a submission at three pieces; the prospectus asks for one to five high-quality images plus an artist statement of 100–300 words and a one-to-two-page bio or CV. Work must be display-ready, framed or wired, and no larger than 36 by 48 inches unless pre-approved. Work created wholly or partially with AI tools must be disclosed at submission and will then be judged on the same criteria as everything else. The gallery insures work on its premises to $5,000 a piece and takes a 30 percent commission on sales; artists deliver and collect their own work at their own cost. No juror is named and no award is offered: the gallery has final authority over inclusion, and what a selected artist gets is the exhibition itself. The call closes 9 November 2026 at 11:59pm Eastern, artists are notified on 13 November, work is dropped off on 30 November, and the exhibition runs 4 December 2026 to 10 January 2027 with an opening reception on 4 December.
