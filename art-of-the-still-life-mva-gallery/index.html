ART OF THE STILL LIFE

MVA Gallery, a commercial gallery in historic Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, runs a rolling programme of themed national group shows, and this one takes the still life. The brief is deliberately wide. The gallery notes that past centuries favoured flowers, fruit, plants, vases, books, candles, shells, mirrors, plates and musical instruments, often loaded with symbolic or moralistic meaning, but it invites work built from anything, however mundane — a pot, a toaster, a clock, a phone, a computer, a lamp, clothing, jewellery, toys. Objects can be presented in their natural state or, through artistic manipulation, debased or elevated. All styles and techniques are welcome, from a straightforward image to an openly experimental one. The gallery shows painting, watercolour, printmaking, mixed media, digital, pastel, encaustic, ink, sculpture, fibre, drawing and photography, and all photographic processes are welcome. Photographs should be framed for a traditional presentation, with black or white frames preferred and white or off-white mats where used, though other mounting is considered. There is no cut-off date for when the work was made. Entry is $35 for three different images, $5 for each additional file up to nine. Work is reviewed by a small rotating committee of artists, gallerists, art reviewers, publishers, curators and academics, who have asked to remain anonymous. Three awards are in play. An Art of Distinction award selects one artist for a featured show in the gallery's Exhibition Room Two at a future date. The Director's Choice Award is $200, granted at the executive director's discretion. The annual Grand Prize goes to one artist chosen from everyone exhibited at MVA across the calendar year, who is offered an exhibition opportunity in New York City and notified in December. The jurors reserve the right not to award any or all prizes. Notification is on or about 12 March 2027 by email, accepted work must reach the gallery by 30 April 2027, and the exhibition runs 5 to 30 May 2027. Artists pay shipping and insurance both ways and must enclose a prepaid return UPS or FedEx label. Framed work should not exceed 48 inches on the longest side or 45 lbs; plexiglass is strongly preferred over glass for shipped pieces. Sales are optional, must list a specific price, and are split 70% to the artist and 30% to the gallery.
