Anja Niedringhaus Courage in Photojournalism Award 2027

Prize $20,000

The IWMF's award for courageous, high-impact photojournalism by women, nonbinary and gender nonconforming photojournalists, created in 2014 with a $1 million gift from the Howard G. Buffett Foundation to honour Anja Niedringhaus, the Pulitzer Prize-winning Associated Press photographer killed in 2014 while covering the elections in Afghanistan. The IWMF frames it as celebrating visual journalistic work that inspires us to take action and compels us to better understand the world. One winner receives a cash prize of $20,000, is publicly honoured and has the work showcased. Entry is free. An application consists of candidate information, a work sample of twelve photographs that best represent the scope and style of the candidate's work in challenging reporting environments — six of which must have been made between 2024 and 2026 — and a candidate statement. Both freelancers and photojournalists affiliated with media organisations are eligible, but journalism must be the candidate's full-time primary profession. A photographer may self-nominate or be nominated by an editor, mentor or journalist peer; a third party can either file a full application or complete an abbreviated nomination form, which does not replace the full submission. Submissions for the 2027 cycle opened 10 November 2026 and close 1 April 2027 at 03:59 UTC, which is 31 March in the Americas, and the IWMF states the winner will be announced in summer 2027. The 2026 awardee was Nicole Tung, with honourable mentions to Daro Sulakauri and Viviana Peretti. The IWMF's own pages have not been rolled forward for this cycle: the award page still presents the 2026 honorees, and the application guidelines and FAQ still read 2026 throughout — the FAQ states the application is open until 31 March 2026, the third-party nomination form carries the same 2026 date, and the recency rule is printed as 2024-2026, which is also the window the organiser's own 2027 submission record repeats. The 2027 dates are published only on that record. The terms are strict on authenticity: photographs must not be manipulated beyond basic colour and contrast correction and minor lightness and darkness adjustments, the IWMF may ask finalists or the winner for the original RAW camera files, and the award follows the NPPA code of ethics. Alongside the summer 2027 wording, the submission platform's timeline also lists 30 September 2027 as the results announcement date.
