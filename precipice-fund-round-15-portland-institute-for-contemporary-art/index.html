Precipice Fund Round 15

Prize $500–$5,000

Since 2013 the Precipice Fund has supported unincorporated visual art collectives, alternative spaces, artist-run initiatives and collaborative projects in and around Portland, Oregon. It is administered by the Portland Institute for Contemporary Art as part of the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts' Regional Regranting Program, and from Round 15 additional support from The Ford Family Foundation extends its capacity to invest in artist-run spaces and independent exhibition projects. Round 15 awards grants of $500 to $5,000, $145,000 in total. Projects must be rooted in the visual arts: interdisciplinary work in performance or other media qualifies only where visual art is clearly central, film is eligible during the creation phase but not for post-production or distribution, and sound art is generally eligible where the work sits within an expanded visual art context. Applications must be collaborative, with two to six collaborators and one named Primary Contact - the fund explicitly does not support the work of an individual artist working alone. The Primary Contact must live in the Portland, Oregon metropolitan area and in Oregon; collaborators may live anywhere. Every funded project must include a meaningful public component, may not be the core programming of a 501(c)(3), and may not be submitted through fiscal sponsorship. Grant funds can cover artist fees and collaborator compensation, exhibition production and installation, materials and fabrication, equipment rental, publication and printing, accessibility services, documentation, venue rental, administration, marketing and project-related transport. Applications typically include a project title and description, a project timeline, a statement of artistic intent, a description of public engagement, collaborator information, a project budget, work samples and any supporting materials. Viewing the pre-recorded Round 15 information session is required before applying, and optional 15-minute conversations with staff are offered afterwards. A panel of artists, curators, organisers, writers and other members of the visual arts community reviews all eligible applications through discussion rather than numerical scoring alone, and may award full or partial funding. Applications opened August 10 and close Sunday September 20, 2026 at 11:59pm PDT, with panel review in early November, notification in mid-November, the public announcement in early December and a grant period running January to December 2027. Free to apply, through Submittable.
