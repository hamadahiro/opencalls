David Whitehouse Research Residency for Artists

Prize Funded residency (travel, room and board)

A funded research residency at one of the world's great glass collections, for artists who want to work in an archive rather than a hot shop. The David Whitehouse Research Residency gives artists dedicated time with the holdings of the Rakow Research Library and the Museum's permanent collection, with support from expert Museum and Library staff. It is explicitly not a glassmaking residency and no provision is made for creating work at The Studio. Residencies run one, two or three weeks and can be scheduled at any point in the following year, with applicants prioritising their own availability on the form. Transportation, room and board are provided, and a residency manager acts as liaison in Corning and at the Museum. Recipients are invited to give a free public lecture at The Studio about their practice or research. Applications go through SlideRoom and need a written proposal of the research plan, 10 digital images of your work, a résumé, an artist statement and two letters of recommendation. Selected applicants are notified in November and the full list of residents is announced each January. The call names no media, but the Museum's published roster of past residents settles it: alongside glassblowers, ceramicists and sculptors it includes Phoebe Stubbs, whose video installations and photographic experiments treat glass as the membrane through which every photographic image is mediated, using blown objects as lenses and diffusers. Past residents have come from Australia, Turkey, Mexico, England and New Zealand.
