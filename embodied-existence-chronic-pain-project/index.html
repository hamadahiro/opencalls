Embodied Existence

Prize Exhibition

The Chronic Pain Project is a 501(c)(3) built on a simple premise — "Chronic pain is invisible. We can change that" — that brings artists living with chronic pain together so their work can do the explaining. Its next exhibition, *Embodied Existence*, asks what it means to be embodied while living with chronic pain, and it hangs at the Watzek Library on the Lewis & Clark College campus in Portland from 9 October to 6 November 2026. Entry is free and the bar is deliberately low: "Art depicting the experience of living with chronic pain. That's it. No specific medium, style, or approach required. Just your truth, told your way." All skill levels are welcome — "You don't need formal training or a gallery resume" — 2D and 3D work are both encouraged, though display space for 3D is limited, and photography sits comfortably inside that. There is a content screen, though: the project does not accept explicit content — "Keep it PG-13 - no drugs, sex, alcohol" — and work "will be turned away if it contains discriminatory themes and gratuitous violence", which is worth weighing if your account of chronic pain runs through medication. Who may enter: anyone 18 or over who lives with chronic pain, or who cares for someone who does. Caregivers are actively wanted and the definition is broad — healthcare providers, family, friends, community members — with the one condition that a caregiver's submission be health-related in some way. The call is local by design: only work that can be dropped off on 8 October and collected on 9 November at the Lewis & Clark campus is accepted, so this is for the Portland metropolitan area. Two submissions per artist are allowed and roughly 10–15 further pieces will fit in the show, so both may not be taken. Work must arrive exhibition-ready and installation-ready — hanging hardware attached, since "work that arrives without proper installation hardware cannot be included" — and the project handles installation at the venue. Nothing is insured: the terms warn that work "may be installed in high-traffic locations or other places with public access and little or no supervision" with "minor theft deterrent elements", and that neither the project nor the exhibition site carries coverage against damage, destruction or theft, so that risk stays with the artist. You upload at least two good photographs of the piece and a short statement; submit through Artwork Archive and also complete the project's Google Form, since both are required. Work will be for sale at the exhibition and in the project's online gallery, with the artist keeping 75% and the project 25%. Artists are optionally invited to record a video about the work for the project's artist-interview series. The deadline is Friday 2 October 2026 and acceptances are confirmed by 6 October. One inconsistency to note: the project's own Upcoming Events page lists the Watzek Library exhibit as running 5 October 2026 – 5 January 2027, while the call document and the entry platform both say 9 October – 6 November; the call's own dates are used here.
