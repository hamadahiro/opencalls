2026 Visual Arts Fund

Prize $10,000

Open only to artists based in the seven-county Twin Cities metro region of Minnesota. The Visual Arts Fund is Midway Contemporary Art's regranting programme, established in 2016 with support from the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, which makes Minneapolis-Saint Paul one of forty-two cities and regions in the Foundation's Regional Regranting Program. In 2026 the fund awards $60,000 as six grants of $10,000 to artist-organised projects. Proposals must name a lead organizer who plays a key role in creating and executing the work and who lives in Hennepin, Scott, Ramsey, Dakota, Anoka, Carver or Washington County; collaborators can live anywhere, individuals or groups may apply, and lead organizers do not need to be US citizens although they need a social security number to be paid. Projects must have a publicly accessible component taking place within the metro region, cannot charge admission or any fee to the public or to participants, and must function primarily within a visual arts context rather than performing arts, music or literature - interdisciplinary elements are fine if the visual arts basis is strong. Examples the fund gives include exhibitions or installations in temporary spaces, new publications, series of events or performances, digital initiatives and public art projects. What it will not fund is an individual artist presenting exclusively their own work, projects that form part of a 501(c)(3)'s programming, projects at a commercial gallery, and anything without free public engagement. 501(c)(3)s, incorporated businesses and students cannot apply, though PhD students can. Photography is fundable and the fund's own grantee archive proves it: Sho Nikaido's BlueSky Photo Workshop received $10,000 for four seasonal, six-week community photography courses held in Japanese restaurants across the Twin Cities. Applications are free, filed through Midway's own portal, and take contact details, a project summary and budget, ten visual support materials (up to ten images, two website URLs and two two-minute videos), a project description answering eight set questions, and bios. A panel of outside jurors scores on artistic excellence, community impact and feasibility. Projects cannot start before 1 January 2027 and must be completed by 30 June 2028; grantees are notified in December 2026 and funding is awarded in January 2027.
