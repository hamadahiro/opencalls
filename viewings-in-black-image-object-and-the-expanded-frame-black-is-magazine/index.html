Viewings in Black: Image, Object, and the Expanded Frame

Black Is Magazine — an archival platform launched by photographer Lia J. Latty to spotlight Black photographers and keep a database of everyone it interviews, run single-handedly from Baltimore — is taking submissions for its second print issue. The theme is work that pushes photography past the flat rectangle: assemblage, collage, photomontage, textile and fibre worked into image-based practice, three-dimensional or sculptural uses of the photograph, and work that moves between photography and another medium in a way that expands what an image can do. The call names its lineage — Romare Bearden, Betye Saar, Ralph Arnold, Lorraine O'Grady, Mickalene Thomas — and says it is looking for work in conversation with Black artistic tradition and cultural history, bringing a distinct point of view to identity, memory, diaspora or representation. In the editors' words, it wants work "for whom the image is not the final destination but a starting point, something to be built upon, layered, folded into object, and transformed." Open to emerging artists across the African diaspora regardless of location, where emerging means artists actively developing a practice who have not yet received significant institutional recognition. Submission is free: up to five work samples (JPEG or PNG, minimum 300 dpi for print), an artist statement of up to 300 words, a bio of up to 150 words and a project proposal of up to 250 words explaining how the work connects to the theme. Submission is through a Google Form, selected artists are notified by email, and the magazine states that every submitting artist receives a response. Separately and year-round, the magazine reviews portfolios and books on an ongoing basis for features on the site — 10 to 15 images from one project at 72 dpi and 1000px on the long side, a project statement and a short bio, by email — and says it particularly seeks work by photographers of the African diaspora who are part of the LGBTQIA+ community.
