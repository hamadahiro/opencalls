GradFoto 2026

Prizes A$1,000 A$500 People's Choice Online exhibition

GradFoto is the Ballarat International Foto Biennale's award for people finishing an art degree, and it has run every year since 2020. The pitch is narrow and useful: you are leaving art school with one resolved project, and this is a free way to put it in front of a serious curator and an audience that is not your cohort. Up to twenty finalists go into an online exhibition on ballaratfoto.org, and the Biennale's stated aim is to show "the high calibre of photographic work by graduates to new audiences across Australia, Aotearoa New Zealand, and beyond, creating connections for them as they begin their artistic careers."

Photography is not inferred here, it is written into the terms: an entry must "be a series of photographic works that have been substantially produced by photographic means, including analogue and digital photography, collage and mixed media". The call also accepts images of artworks that engage photography, installation documentation and photobook documentation as part of the submission, so a project that ends up as an object rather than a set of prints is not shut out.

The gate is a list, not a map. You must be 18 or over at the closing date, graduating a diploma, bachelor, honours or masters programme in 2026, and enrolled at one of about forty named institutions in Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand - ANU, RMIT, Monash, VCA, UNSW, UTS, Photography Studies College, Collarts, National Art School, Elam, AUT, Massey, Whitecliffe, Dunedin School of Art and others. Count the lists before you trust one. The call page and the form's dropdown each name exactly 40 institutions and agree name for name, but the terms and conditions - the document you tick to agree to - name 38: Sydney College of the Arts and TAFE Queensland are selectable in the dropdown and absent from the terms, which also fold Elam and the Ilam School into plainer "University of Auckland" and "University of Canterbury School of Fine Arts" entries. For those two schools that is worth an email first, because the terms give the Biennale and the judges "absolute discretion in determining the eligibility of an entry based on their interpretation of the Terms and Conditions of entry". If your school is on neither list, the Biennale asks you to email its Education & Public Engagement Coordinator about being added for a future round rather than entering now.

The judge is Bindi Vora - an interdisciplinary artist, Senior Curator at Autograph in London and Associate Lecturer at UAL, whose curating works through postcolonial histories, diaspora and reinterpretation in lens-based practice, and who has written for FOAM and the British Journal of Photography. She selects up to twenty finalists from the files you upload; the overall A$1,000 prize and the public-voted A$500 People's Choice are then decided from the exhibited work, which means the sequence you submit is the sequence that competes.

Practicalities. Entry is free, and the form has no payment step. Submit between 1 and 20 jpegs at 2000 px on the longest side, 300 dpi, 2-10 MB each, named with your name, the series title and a sequence number, with one marked _HERO for the Biennale's social posts. Nothing watermarked is accepted. The project must be finished and must have been made or resolved after 1 January 2026. The upload widget is the cap made concrete - "Max. files: 20", "You may upload 1-20 images" - and alongside it sits a video field that neither the call page nor the terms mentions: up to five minutes of video work, shared as a link to Google Drive, Dropbox or similar with the sharing settings turned on. You also supply a project statement and a biography, each capped at 200 words, and the Biennale says it will edit artist statements for consistency before publishing them.

Read the rights clause before you enter. Copyright stays with you, but every finalist grants the Biennale, its touring partners and its sponsors a non-exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free licence to use the work, your likeness and your name - with attribution - to promote the award and the Biennale, in this round and in future years, and with no fee or royalty payable. That is promotional scope rather than a general assignment, but it does not expire.

Dates: entries close 11:59 pm AEDT on 22 November 2026, finalists are contacted by 10 December, the online exhibition opens and the winner is announced on 11 December 2026, public voting runs to 11:59 pm on 1 February 2027, and the People's Choice winner is announced on Friday 5 February 2027. Ballarat Foto states it will take applications in alternative formats to improve accessibility - there is an email and a phone number on the page for arranging that.
