The Deets: A National Exhibition of Intricate and Heavily Detailed Artworks

Prizes $300 Best in Show Exhibition

The Vault is exactly what it sounds like: a bank vault inside d'Art Center's building on Boush Street, kept as the gallery's small room. The Deets is built for it — a show of work dense enough to hold someone at arm's length, which is a specification about the viewing distance rather than about any one medium.

That makes it a better fit than most all-media calls for photographers whose work lives in the grain: contact-printed large format, cyanotype and gum surfaces, collaged or hand-embellished prints, anything where the second minute of looking finds something the first did not. Nothing is excluded, and the eligibility text commits only to “all styles and aesthetics are considered”.

Entry is $30 for three slots and $5 for each work after that, to a maximum of ten. One award, Best in Show at $300, chosen not by an outside juror but by a panel of d'Art's own staff and resident artists, with Gallery Director Amanda Bradley curating the selection. Work is capped at 36 inches in its longest dimension. The prospectus will waive that by prior arrangement, but only in proportion to how much of the installation you take on yourself.

The costs after acceptance are the thing to read twice: 40% gallery commission on any sale, artist-paid shipping both ways, and a mandatory prepaid FedEx or UPS return label, since d'Art will not ship by USPS or accept freight. Work must be in Norfolk by 5pm on 19 December 2026, and the show opens 9 January 2027 with a reception on the 14th shared with Portrayed next door.
