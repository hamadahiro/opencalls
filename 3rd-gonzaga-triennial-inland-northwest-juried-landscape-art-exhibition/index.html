3rd Gonzaga Triennial: Inland Northwest Juried Landscape Art Exhibition

Prizes $1,000 First Place $500 Second Place $250 Honorable Mention

The Jundt Art Museum sits on the Gonzaga University campus in Spokane and runs this juried landscape exhibition once every three years. The 3rd Gonzaga Triennial occupies the Jundt Galleries and the Arcade Gallery from May 22 to August 28, 2027, with the reception and awards on the Friday evening before, May 21, 4-7pm. The stated purpose is to show the range of contemporary art being made in the Inland Northwest and its relationship to the local landscape, and the prospectus builds the theme out of quotations from Chief Seattle, Spokane Garry, James Thomson and W. J. T. Mitchell rather than a single brief.

Media is open: work "may be in any media", and there are no specific size limits for 2D or 3D pieces, though the museum notes gallery space is limited. Everything submitted must be a completed work - no incomplete pieces, conceptual works or project proposals - and must be exhibition-ready with all its own hardware. It is worth knowing the recent history: the museum names a medium for five of its past award winners and all five worked in paint or mixed media - the 2018 and 2024 first, second and third places - while stating no medium at all for its six honorable mentions.

Two jurors decide: Wes Jessup, Executive Director of the Northwest Museum of Arts and Culture in Spokane and a museum professional with over 30 years of experience, and Paul Manoguerra, Director and Curator of the Jundt Art Museum since 2013 and a juror for the first two Triennials. They judge on artistic quality and merit, adherence to the landscape theme, the artist's educational and professional achievements, and observance of the application rules. First prize is $1,000, second $500, and any honorable mentions receive $250; the museum says sponsored awards might also be available.

The eligibility rule is the hard gate and it is not negotiable. Artists must live in one of twenty named Washington counties or ten named Idaho counties in the Inland Northwest, and must upload documentary proof of residency - a mortgage or rental statement, driver's license or utility bill, under 2MB - as part of the application. The prospectus states that no exceptions will be made. Each artist also uploads a biography covering major educational and professional achievements.

Entry is a one-time $25 fee through CaFÉ, for up to three works with one image each showing the entire piece. The deadline is Monday, February 1, 2027, 10:59pm PST, with acceptances announced February 10, 2027, publication-quality images due March 1, and a list of up to 20 postcard addresses due April 1.

Two things to plan for. Work is not for sale in this exhibition and has to stay on site for the full run. And handling is on the artist at both ends: delivery to the museum loading dock between 10am Wednesday May 12 and 4pm Friday May 14, 2027, and removal between August 30 and 31, 2027, after which unclaimed work is considered abandoned and becomes museum property. Gonzaga insures all work in the museum's custody, and the museum does all installation, with location, placement and lighting entirely at its discretion.
