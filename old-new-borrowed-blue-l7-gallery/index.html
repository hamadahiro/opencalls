Old, New, Borrowed, Blue

Prizes $300 Exhibition

L7 Gallery is an independent contemporary gallery run from Bellingham, Washington by curator Jo Ford (BFA San Francisco Art Institute, MFA UC Berkeley) and Blaise Smith, which presents its juried exhibitions online. For its October 2026 exhibit the gallery invites artists to respond to the four themes in the traditional Victorian rhyme "Something old, something new, something borrowed, something blue" — a familiar wedding poem whose origin, the gallery notes, lay in magical charms used to ward off evil and protect future generations. It asks what those words could possibly mean right now, "in a world of cultural recycling, generative AI, overwhelming tech capital, political and ecological anxiety, loneliness, isolation and the existential fear of the future". OLD is offered as history, tradition, nostalgia, forgotten or rediscovered stories and objects that reappear from the past; NEW as an experiment, a new medium, a new approach or collaboration; BORROWED as found material, borrowed identities, inspirations from channelled entities and ideas drawn from cultural interactions; BLUE as the colour itself, sadness, longing, distance, water, sky, bruises, dreams, loss, hope — whatever blue means to you. Artists may respond to one or all four categories with new or existing work, and the gallery says it is especially interested in work that is personal, strange, uncomfortable, beautiful, experimental, craft or concept oriented (or both), or simply difficult to categorise. Photography is named in the accepted media alongside painting, drawing, watercolour, sculpture, digital art, printmaking and fabric art. Open to artists 18 and over; $30 covers one to three images. The exhibition runs 10 October to 10 November 2026 and is juried by Hannah Sage Kay, an arts writer and critic based between New York and Los Angeles who has contributed to ARTnews, The Art Newspaper, Autre, BOMB, The Brooklyn Rail, the Financial Times, The Guardian, the Los Angeles Review of Books and Nka Journal of Contemporary African Art. One winner receives a $300 cash award and two further artists receive honourable mentions from the juror. All work must be for sale for the run of the show and cannot be withdrawn before it ends; artists receive 60% of the sale price, and prices set at application cannot be changed later.
