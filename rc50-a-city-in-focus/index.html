RC50: A City in Focus

Prizes $200 stipend Exhibition

Free call from the City of Rancho Cucamonga's RC Public Art Program for a citywide photography exhibition marking the city's 50th anniversary. An Art Selection Panel chosen by the City will pick 50 photographs, each digitally applied to display banners by a third-party vendor and installed in public spaces around Rancho Cucamonga through the anniversary year; selected images may also appear on the project's own website. Every selected photographer is paid a $200 stipend. The City invites photographers of all experience levels and the themes are left to the artist, framed around four prompts: Community (people and everyday moments, neighbourhoods and landmarks, celebrations and traditions), Rancho Cucamonga history, the topography of the land, and nature native to the region. One entry per applicant. Alongside the image, the application asks for a Community Connection Statement of up to 100 words answering "What does Rancho Cucamonga mean to you?" and a three-word completion of the prompt "My Rancho Cucamonga is…"; the statement is scored on how well it communicates the applicant's connection to the city and how closely the work meets the call's goals. Entry is free and the CaFÉ system carries no charge. Read four things before applying. First, the deadline can arrive early: the City states the window closes December 3, 2026 at 11:59 p.m. PDT "or earlier if 200 applications are received (whichever occurs first)." Second, the City's own prospectus states the geographic bar two different ways on the same page — the Artist Eligibility list says "Artist must reside in the Inland Empire region," while the Geographic Eligibility paragraph says "This is an open call, but preference will be given to applicants who work, reside, or attend school in the Inland Empire Region." Third, the rights terms: the artist retains copyright, but the City "owns all installed artwork and reserves the right to copy, reproduce and adjust the artwork as it sees fit, in perpetuity," and reserves the right to remove, replace or adjust the work for the duration of the project. Fourth, the display is not guaranteed in place or duration — banners go onto functioning equipment that may need repair or replacement, and the City issues "no guarantee… for the location or longevity of display." Content limits: no political statements or persons, no religious references or symbols, no copyrighted or trademarked material, no corporate branding, nothing offensive or obscene, and work must be appropriate for public display in civic spaces. Only original photographs are accepted and applicants pledge the image was not digitally altered from someone else's work; artists are responsible for obtaining consent from identifiable people in their photographs. Open to all ages, with parent or guardian permission for minors. Current City employees may not apply. Judging runs January 4–25, 2027 and artists are notified by February 1, 2027.
