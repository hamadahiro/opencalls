The Room Next Door

Online juried photography exhibition from Decagon Gallery on the space just beyond what we see — a door left ajar, a window lit across the courtyard, a hallway that turns out of sight, a voice through the wall. The gallery is looking for photographs that bring the viewer to the edge of a space they cannot fully enter: images where something is felt more than shown, where the frame stops just short of an answer, and where what lies beyond is left for the viewer to imagine. The organisers put the distinction plainly — the mystery should come from what the photograph withholds, not from what it hides. Thresholds, doorways, windows and reflections are welcome, as are interiors seen from outside and exteriors seen from within, neighbouring lives glimpsed at a distance, sounds, light and shadows arriving from somewhere else, and constructed or staged work that invents what waits on the other side. Documentary, conceptual and experimental approaches are all accepted; people may appear, but the subject is the space between here and there. Open to photographers worldwide aged 18 and over, with all photographic processes and styles welcome. First place receives $500, second $250, third $150 and two honorable mentions $50 each — $1,000 total in cash awards — plus a Human Presence Award granting free entry to a future exhibition of the recipient's choice; cash awards are limited to one per photographer, and a photographer selected for more than one receives only the award of greatest value. Selected artists get an online exhibition by Decagon Gallery, publication in a professionally designed exhibition catalog, and digital badges and certificates. Entry is $25 for up to 4 photographs and $5 each additional, with 10 image slots on the entry form. The juror is Dr. Susan Bright, the Australian/British curator, art historian, writer and lecturer. Notification is January 1, 2027 and the exhibition runs January 10 to March 10, 2027. Photographers experiencing financial hardship, living in a war-torn area or residing in a sanctioned country can ask the gallery to waive the entry fee.
