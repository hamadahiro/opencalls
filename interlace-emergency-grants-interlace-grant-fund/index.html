Interlace Emergency Grants

Prize $50–$500

Interlace Emergency Grants are small, fast grants for Providence-area visual artists facing an emergency — the fund names eviction, a utility shut-off, healthcare expenses and a phone or internet cut-off among the situations it exists for. Awards run from $50 to $500 out of a pool of $1,250 dispersed every month; if a month's qualified requests come to less than the pool, the remainder rolls forward. The programme never closes, but it runs on a published monthly cycle rather than one competition: applications are due on the 7th of each month, applicants are notified by the 21st, and funds are disbursed in the final week. Artists may apply as often as they need within a calendar year up to a $500 ceiling, so a photographer who received $100 one month can come back for the rest, and an unsuccessful applicant may simply reapply. Applications go through Submittable, which requires a free account that Interlace says it uses to protect applicants' privacy; they are anonymised by Interlace staff before review and read by an independent group of local arts, education and community-service professionals. Recipients are not publicly announced, and the money is unrestricted with no reporting requirements. To be eligible an applicant must self-identify as a visual artist, be over 18 at the time of applying, have lived in Rhode Island for at least a year within a 10-mile radius of the City of Providence (the fund is explicit that the radius does not extend into Massachusetts), and not be a full-time student or enrolled in a degree programme. Emergency Grant applicants do not need to be US citizens. Interlace publishes its whole question set as a downloadable PDF: the application asks for a description of the emergency in up to 300 words (or a two-minute audio or video recording instead), the amount requested, a work sample of three to five images or a CV, resume or short video — or a portfolio link in place of files — and, separately, a link to an artist website, a public social account with a reasonable history of current work or an article verifying the practice; those who can supply neither are invited to get in touch instead. An applicant must also be able to receive a cheque in their legal name, and anyone employed by or related to the partnering institutions' staff, residents or board is barred. The fund asks applicants to use the honour system and apply only where livelihood or well-being is genuinely affected. Interlace is backed by the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts and partners in Providence.
