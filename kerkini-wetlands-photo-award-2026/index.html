Kerkini Wetlands Photo Award 2026

A new annual international photography award dedicated to Lake Kerkini in Northern Greece, a Ramsar-listed wetland with more than 300 bird species and, in the organiser's words, one of Europe's premier destinations for wildlife and bird photography. The 2026 edition is the first, organised by Agkistro Action, a social cooperative in the Serres region, and it is built as a conservation platform rather than a tourism promotion: the stated aim is to build a lasting visual archive of the lake and to promote environmental awareness through photography. Four categories - Birds of Kerkini, Wildlife of the Wetland, Wetland Landscape, and Human & Wetland - and the one hard rule is geographic: every photograph must have been taken within Lake Kerkini National Park or its surrounding wetland ecosystem, though there is no restriction on when it was taken, so archive work from earlier visits qualifies. Open to amateur and professional photographers worldwide aged 18 and over; entrants aged 18 to 25 are automatically considered for the Young Photographer of the Year Award. The entry fee is €15 per participant for up to 10 photographs across one or more categories, submitted as JPEGs through Picter. The prize fund is €3,000: €1,000 for Photo of the Year, €400 for each of the four category winners, and €400 for the Young Photographer award, plus trophies, up to five special mentions, and a place in the official exhibition for winning and shortlisted work. The jury is Costas Vidakis, Ellie Rothnie, Emil Enchev and Nikos Petrou, judging anonymously. The rules run to a detailed ethical-wildlife code (no baiting, no captive animals, no sky replacement or compositing) and ban images generated or altered with AI, while AI-based noise reduction and sharpening are allowed. Photographers keep copyright and grant only a non-exclusive, non-commercial, credited licence tied to the award. Submissions run 20 September to 20 November 2026; finalists are announced on 5 January 2027 and winners at the awards ceremony on World Wetlands Day, 2 February 2027.
