Carroll Harris Simms National Black Art Competition and Exhibition

This is one of the longest-running competitions for Black artists in the United States and photography is a named category, not an afterthought. The African American Museum in Dallas started it in 1976 as an annual for southwest Black artists, partly because the museum needed to build a collection and partly because Black artists needed somewhere to show; it became a biennial, and in 1999 the board named it for Carroll Harris Simms, for his outstanding contribution to art and art education. Previous winners include Sedrick Huckaby, Jeremy Biggers, Charley Palmer, Florence Staats, Asia Youngs-Bailey, Lee Ransaw and Leamon Green.

The structure is six parallel $1,000 awards — painting, sculpture, mixed media, drawing, printmaking and photography — so a photographer is competing against other photographers rather than against a painting. One of the six category winners is then named Best in Show and receives a solo exhibition at the museum within the following two years; the record's summary says that artist agrees to donate a work from the show to the permanent collection, while the legal agreement calls the donation voluntary and encouraged. That is a museum solo and an accession, which is a different order of outcome from most $25 open calls. Eligibility is Black artists residing in the United States; the portal allows up to three separate applications while the legal agreement caps entries at two artworks, and AI-generated art and photography are refused outright.

Practicalities: $25 entry, one to four JPEG images per work at 300 dpi and no more than 5 MB each, numbered sequentially, with medium, dimensions, completion date, an artist statement and current biographical information; images embedded in PDFs are rejected. Sculpture takes one to four views of the same piece. The deadline is 27 November 2026, notifications go out 6–12 December, and accepted work must reach the museum by 17 January 2027 — once it has arrived it cannot be withdrawn from the exhibition. No juror is named on any published surface. Note also that the museum’s own site carries no page for this cycle’s call: the only Carroll Harris Simms page on aamdallas.org is an RSVP form for the 28th Biennial’s opening, with no dates on it, while the museum’s own prospectus calls this competition the 29th biennial — so both the dated detail above and the link above go to the museum-authored CaFÉ record.
