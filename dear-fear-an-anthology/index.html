Dear Fear: An Anthology

Dear Fear is an anthology of letters written to "fear foods" — the foods and drinks that trigger intense anxiety, guilt, panic or distress — by people in eating disorder recovery, with images by contributing visual artists set alongside poems, essays and other creative nonfiction. The editors chose the constrained form deliberately, merging the epistle with the apostrophe (an address to a non-living thing) to bring their writers' voices into the same room, where they can hear one another beyond the characteristics that culture says should divide them. The call welcomes emerging writers and artists and particularly encourages submissions from communities that have historically faced resistance to representation, including LGBT+, BIPOC and disabled communities; the editors also note the relative lack of literature by men about eating disorders and recovery, and hope to make space for those voices. Visual artists and makers of hybrid work may submit up to five images, as JPG/JPEG only, 1,200 pixels on the longest side, sRGB, named FirstName_LastName_1.jpg; prose and poetry are submitted through Duotrope and visual work through a Google form that requires signing in to a Google account. Be aware that the organisers enumerate no media at all — the category is simply "Visual Art and Hybrid Work", and the word photograph appears nowhere on the submission page, the anthology's own Duotrope listing or the rest of the site; nothing excludes photography either, and the file specification admits it. What signals the visual intent is the masthead: the visual editor is Roula Seikaly, described on the anthology's own site as an independent curator, writer and Co-Director at Humble Arts Foundation whose curatorial practice addresses contemporary photography and new media, and who has curated exhibitions at SF Camerawork, Blue Sky Gallery, Colorado Photographic Arts Center and Photographic Center Northwest. The poetry editor is Danielle Coffyn and the nonfiction editor is Maggie Russell. Hybrid works involving video, music or other streaming content are not accepted at this time, and material generated by artificial intelligence cannot be accepted. Previously published work is ineligible, though the editors do not count work posted on a platform you control — a personal blog, newsletter or social account — as published; simultaneous submissions are welcome with prompt notice if the work is taken elsewhere. The submission fee is $3 and is waived on request, and optional editorial feedback from the editors costs $30; all fees go towards paying contributors, and accepted work receives what the editors call a modest honorarium, which they do not quantify. They aim to respond within three months, and will make accommodations for submissions from justice-involved or unhoused writers and artists, and from people submitting on their behalf. The submission window runs 1 August to 15 October 2026, and the anthology is anticipated to publish in February 2027 with Sheila-Na-Gig, a nonprofit poetry journal and small press.
