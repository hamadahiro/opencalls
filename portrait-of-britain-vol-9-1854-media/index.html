Portrait of Britain Vol. 9

Prize UK-wide JCDecaux screen exhibition

The ninth volume of Portrait of Britain, the annual award run by 1854 Media, publisher of the British Journal of Photography, and billed by the organiser as "the UK's biggest annual photography exhibition". Winning portraits are shown up and down the country for a month on JCDecaux UK digital screens, and winners also get a chance to be featured in the British Journal of Photography and have their work sent to 1854's national and international press list — past volumes have been picked up by the Guardian, the Independent, the BBC, Sky News, Aesthetica and It's Nice That. The archive on 1854's own site shows the recent pattern: a shortlist of 200 photographs, from which 100 winning images are drawn. There is no cash prize. The Vol. 9 jury is Vanley Burke, Adama Jalloh, Ingrid Pollard, Mimi Mollica and Kalpesh Lathigra (photographers), Anna Liesching (art curator, National Museums NI), Dr Ajamu X (artist and archive curator), Francis Hodgson (founder, Prix Pictet), Tom Booth Woodger (publisher, Bluecoat Press) and Mick Moore, Zoe Harrison and Sinead Solomon of the British Journal of Photography. The award is open to professional and non-professional photographers of all ages and to international as well as UK-based entrants, but the restriction is on the picture, not the photographer: the image itself must have been shot in the UK (England, Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland) after 1 January 2025, and may be of an individual or a group. Entries are single images, JPEG only, up to 10MB each, with a title under 200 characters and a description under 2,000 characters. Written consent from the subject (or a parent or guardian for a minor) is required to appear in the outdoor exhibition; verbal permission is enough for the shortlist. Entry is priced per image slot — £10 for one, £20 for three, £35 for ten, £60 for twenty — and 1854 Access Members (Full or Digital) can submit ten images free as part of their membership, though they must stay members through judging and the exhibition for their entries to remain eligible. Fees are non-refundable. The cutoff is not end of day in every zone: the terms state "The deadline for entries is 5th November 2026 – 23:59 (UK Time)". No copyright is transferred, but entrants grant 1854 and JCDecaux the right to display and publish entries without payment for the purposes of the award and its publicity. On AI the terms are explicit: "We accept entries that consist of original photographs modified by AI. However, all significant AI modifications must be clearly indicated in the caption."
