Open Call for 2028 Exhibitions

Prizes Exhibition W.A.G.E. artist fee Production budget

Most $15 open calls buy you a chance to hang something. This one buys a chance at a produced show. Open Source Gallery is W.A.G.E.-certified, which is not decoration — it means the artist fee is set against a published schedule rather than at the gallery's discretion, and the gallery states that selected artists also receive a production budget plus help with planning, production and communications. For a photographer with a project that needs to be made rather than simply framed, that last part is the rare bit.

The brief is open on medium and open on geography — “artists working in any medium and location” — but it is not open on sensibility. The gallery is asking for practices that emphasise experimentation, sustainability and social engagement, and its own programme is conceptually-driven work shown monthly in a renovated Brooklyn carriage house whose large doors open onto the street. A proposal that ignores the room will read as a portfolio mailed to the wrong address; the gallery publishes its floor plan precisely so you don't have to guess.

On the money: the $15 is structured as a donation and paid through a dedicated link that offers exactly one preset amount, labelled “$15 (Required Application Fee)”, its only alternative being a box for increasing the figure — so there is nothing cheaper to find, and PayPal is there for applicants outside the US. The receipt uploads with the application. The waiver sentence is worth knowing because it is missing from the one page most applicants will read: the call page says nothing about it, while the application form and the payment screen both say the gallery's programmes are always free or pay-what-you-can and invite anyone for whom $15 is cost-prohibitive to write and work out an accommodation.

Everything goes in one PDF: a proposal under 1,000 words, a statement under 250, a CV, and 10 to 15 images. No juror is named and the gallery says only that applicants will hear in early 2027, so this is a slow decision on a 2028 slot — plan the project on that timeline, not on a spring turnaround.
