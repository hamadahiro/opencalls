Grants to Individuals - 2027 Cycle

Prize $5,000-$20,000

The Graham Foundation for Advanced Studies in the Fine Arts has funded independent work on architecture and the designed environment from its Chicago base since 1956, and its Grants to Individuals programme is one of the few places a self-directed photographer can get a book, an exhibition or a long research trip paid for outright. Two grant types are on offer. Production and Presentation grants cover the cost of realising and publicly presenting a project - exhibitions, installations, films, videos, new media and web-based initiatives, publications and other public programmes - and do not exceed $20,000, with awards generally running $5,000 to $15,000; these require a committed producer or presenting partner already in place when the inquiry form goes in. Research and Development grants cover travel, archival research, documentation, materials and supplies, do not exceed $10,000 and generally run $5,000 to $10,000, and need no producer. The Foundation names no medium, and its own grantee roster settles the question: Kate Joyce holds a 2026 publication grant for Watt or Fall, made across a year-long residency photographing Fallingwater, a building the Foundation's own citation calls one that defies its visual representation; Noritaka Minami has been funded three times, most recently in 2025 for a photographic study of the concrete pillboxes of Hokkaido; and Zoe Leonard (Al Rio/To the River), Camilo Jose Vergara (Tracking Newark, N.J., 1977-2024), Ashley Hunt (a photographic survey of more than 250 US prisons), David Schalliol, George Legrady, Leonardo Finotti and Jason Oddy have all been funded as individuals for bodies of photographic work on the built environment. What the Foundation wants is a project that creates and communicates significant ideas about architecture and the designed environment, takes a position, brings work into the public realm and amplifies emerging and underrecognised perspectives - first-time applicants get particular attention. Grantmaking focuses on the United States but the Foundation also awards internationally; funded projects must include meaningful public dissemination in English. Work done in pursuit of an academic degree is not funded (advanced doctoral candidates go to the separate Carter Manny Award instead), only one grant application may be made per year, and no more than 30 per cent of the request may go to the applicant's own fee. Applying is free and two-stage: an inquiry form by 15 September 2026 at 5 pm Central, invitations to submit a full proposal in December 2026 to January 2027, proposals due February 2027, decisions 31 May 2027 and the earliest project start date 15 June 2027.
