photoED Magazine — Objects (Issue #79)

photoED, the Canadian photography magazine, is building Issue #79 around still life: photographers exploring objects with unique, lens-based methods. The call asks for fine art photography projects that present objects in unusual compositions — the magazine's own framing runs from the historic repertoire of flowers, fruit, plants, vases, books, candles, shells, mirrors, plates and musical instruments to anything mundane that an artist's eye picks out. What it does not want is stock photography, and it does not want commissions: existing projects and finished works only. The editors ask for well considered submissions — a project or idea that has been laboured over — and diverse interpretations of the theme. Entries are blind reviewed by the magazine's volunteer curatorial editorial board, and judges assess how the work presents a unique reading of the subject rather than how technically polished a single frame is. The magazine recommends reading a full print or digital replica of a recent edition before submitting, to work out whether the work is a fit. Entry is limited to eight images and one submission per person. Files should be at least 2000 pixels on the longest side and no larger than 10 MB each, and every filename must carry the photographer's first and last name. Submitting transfers no rights: the photographer keeps full ownership, the magazine asks for no exclusivity, and the same images can go to any other publication, project or exhibition. Selected contributors are published in photoED's print and digital replica editions, and those chosen for print receive printed copies with worldwide shipping. Additional selected contributors may feature in the free-access Digital Extra companion editions, and all contributors are promoted across the magazine's email campaigns and social channels. Entry is free for paid Patreon patrons and paid print subscribers, who also receive a copy of the edition automatically when it appears in April 2027; the general fee is $20 CAD, or $30 within Canada and $35 internationally if an entrant opts to pre-purchase a single print copy as part of the fee. All entrants may opt in to free digital replica access. The editor will waive the fee for economically challenged artists on request. Entries are welcome worldwide, with the exception of residents of Russia, Belarus and sanctioned countries.
