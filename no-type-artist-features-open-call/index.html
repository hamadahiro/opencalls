No-Type Artist Features — Open Call

Standing, free open call from No-Type, the independent Chicago photography publisher, for its Artist Features programme. The editors are "looking for new and exciting bodies of work from emerging and established photographers alike", and there are two outcomes: a FEATURE, which is a post carrying the work and the artist statement on No-Type's website and social media, or an INTERVIEW, which is everything in a feature plus a conversation about the artist's practice with editor Dylan Yarbrough. READ THIS BEFORE APPLYING: this call is not the printed magazine. The guidelines are titled "Artist Features" and the licence an applicant grants covers reproduction "on our social media and website as well as for related promotion and archival purposes" — No-Type's themed print issues run as separate, paid calls. There is no cash prize and no exhibition; what is on offer is a curated editorial feature from a publisher whose issues are real printed objects, at no cost to the photographer. Submissions are open on a rolling basis with no fixed deadline, reviewed monthly, and selected artists are contacted directly; the volume of submissions means no individual feedback on work that is not selected. Open to lens-based artists aged 18 and over from any country, emerging or established. Work must be made through a photographic or lens-based process — film, digital capture, archival materials, collage and alternative photographic methods are all named — while AI-generated images or work produced primarily through generative technologies are not eligible. Submit 15 to 20 images as a public link to Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive or similar, plus a single PDF combining an 80-120 word third-person bio and a 150-250 word statement; JPG, under 10 MB each, 2000 px on the longest side. Multiple submissions are permitted. Artists retain full copyright and are fully credited, and No-Type undertakes not to sell, license or distribute the images for any unrelated purpose without permission. The editors also state they will not edit or alter submitted images, though an image may appear as a cropped detail in a thumbnail or page design.
