ArtDepå 2026

Prize Exhibition at Gröna Rummet, Konstepidemin, with works offered for sale (30% commission)

ArtDepå begins from a premise most open calls ignore: the work already exists. Almost every artist keeps an archive in the studio — sketches, tryouts, pieces made for a public or private commission, things shown once, things that never found a gallery. ArtDepå is built to put those works back in front of people, and to sell them.

The first edition runs 4–13 December 2026 in Gröna Rummet at Konstepidemin, the large artists' complex in Gothenburg. The hang is deliberately dense and maximalist — walls, tables, stands and vitrines — designed for browsing and for the unexpected find rather than for the measured spacing of a white cube.

Photography is named outright among the welcome media, with painting, drawing, printmaking, sculpture and objects, ceramics, textile, jewellery and other physical work that suits the format. The call is for professional artists; Gothenburg and its surrounding municipalities are particularly encouraged, but applications are welcome from anywhere.

Twenty artists are selected by a jury — Mona Wallström and Ami Norda, with Jelizaveta Suska as selection coordinator — weighing artistic quality and a spread of media and approaches that fits ArtDepå's character. The application form names all three jurors; the call page on Konstepidemin's site names only two. Be aware the organisers' own page says 30 artists in its opening description and 20 in its Selection section; the Selection figure is the one stated as the outcome of the jury process.

It is free: no application fee and no participation fee. ArtDepå takes 30% of any sale, and every work shown must be for sale, labelled with the artist's name, the title, the artist's contact and the price. If you are selected you may bring up to a hundred works, which is what makes this a genuine clear-out of the archive rather than a two-piece group show.

What you owe in return is logistics and risk. Artists arrange their own transport and packaging, make sure work is ready to install, and apply the printed labels on delivery. The organiser provides no insurance and, under the participation agreement, is not responsible for theft, loss or damage other than in cases of gross negligence — so budget for your own cover if the work warrants it.

The application wants a one-page CV, a 500-character biography, a 500-character artist statement, five images of representative works with full captions, links to your website and Instagram, and an estimate of how many works you would bring. Konstepidemin's page also lists F-Tax registration — a Swedish business tax registration — among the required items, but the form itself does not ask for it, so it need not stop you applying from abroad; it reads as something that would matter when a work sells.

Applications close 4 October 2026 and late applications are not considered. Selected artists hear back on 14 October, the final artwork list is due 17 November, delivery runs 24–29 November, and unsold works are collected on 13 December.
