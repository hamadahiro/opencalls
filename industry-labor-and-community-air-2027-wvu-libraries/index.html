Industry, Labor, and Community AIR 2027

Prizes $1,000 materials Up to $3,500 travel, lodging and per diem $1,000 honorarium Exhibition at WVU Downtown Library

The Art in the Libraries Program at West Virginia University Libraries runs project-based artist residencies built on the holdings of the West Virginia & Regional History Center. Earlier cycles worked with the West Virginia Feminist Activist Collection; the 2027 cycle turns to the Industry, Labor, and Community collections under the theme Common Threads: Industry, Labor & Community.

The material is the draw: union records, company-town archives, oral histories and grassroots community papers documenting local enterprise, working-class history, union organisation and, in the programme's own words, the human story behind West Virginia's industrial footprint. Artists are asked to activate it — visual works, installations, publications, performances or other interpretive expressions that spark dialogue around place, labour, solidarity and heritage.

Photography is not an inference here. It appears in the accepted-media list alongside painting, drawing, sculpture, collage, digital art, book arts, ceramics, textiles, audio/oral-history installation and performance; and the organiser's list of example outcomes names a photographic series developed in response to archival images, either recreating them in contemporary settings or drawing thematic connections between past and present. Documentary and archival practices are the natural fit.

Funding is in three parts: $1,000 for art and exhibit materials awarded on signed contract, up to $3,500 in reimbursements for transportation, lodging and per diem meals, and a $1,000 honorarium paid on successful completion. Ownership of the completed work remains entirely with the artist.

The commitment is a five-to-seven day stay in Morgantown in March or April 2027, with a minimum of two full days in the WVRHC examining the collections, a work created in response by an agreed deadline, and a 20-minute community presentation on process and creative intention. Project outcomes are shown primarily in the WVU Downtown Library, with the public programme and exhibition launch in April 2027.

Selection is by the WVU Art in the Libraries Committee together with WVRHC archivists and guest juror Rachel Johnson, Arts and Culture Specialist at the Morgantown Arts Council, on readiness to engage with archival material, alignment with the programme's public-engagement goals, artistic integrity, professional track record and budget feasibility.

One inconsistency worth reading before you apply: the programme description says the residency is open to all U.S. and Canadian-based artists, while the Application Process section says artists, curators and creators aged 18+ based in the United States. The eligibility block is the one that governs, so Canadian applicants should confirm with the curator first. WVU alumni and underrepresented artists are especially encouraged, but neither is a bar.

There is no application fee. Applications are a single combined PDF emailed to the Art in the Libraries curator: a 150-word abstract, a 500-word statement of interest naming the specific collections you intend to research, a 500-word timeline and budget up to $1,000, a 200-word artist statement including any connection to Appalachia, a CV of no more than two pages, and 5–10 high-resolution images at 300 dpi or web links. Applications opened October 1, 2026 and close November 30, 2026; applicants are notified between December 7 and 30, 2026.
