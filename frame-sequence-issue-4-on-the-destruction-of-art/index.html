FRAME/SEQUENCE Issue 4 — On the Destruction of Art

Prizes Publication Cash award per accepted piece

FRAME/SEQUENCE is a Philadelphia print periodical founded in 2025 by photographer and educator Lily Colman (RISD MFA, 2020) as a publishing non-profit under the fiscal sponsorship of CultureWorks of Philadelphia, spotlighting underrepresented and emerging photographers, writers, artists and communities across Philadelphia and Greater Pennsylvania. Issue 4, due out as the March 2027 periodical, takes the theme "On the Destruction of Art": what it means to make work in an age of cultural and societal destruction, how artists keep working in those conditions, and how a society responds when art itself is debased, degraded or commodified. The editors want work that uses destruction or ephemerality as method — rupture and renewal — alongside writing that reads art's place in culture through an art-historical or contemporary lens, and they say explicitly that they are looking for everything from photographers working on the ground in real time to more interpretive photographic and written responses. The regional frame is relaxed for this issue: the periodical still focuses on Philadelphia, but submissions for Issue 4 are open to any artist or writer, and former University of the Arts students forced to transfer when that school closed, along with their professors, are especially encouraged. Photography, creative writing (poetry, fiction, creative nonfiction), visual art, mixed media and community-focused projects are accepted, and all visual art must incorporate photography in some way. Submit between five and ten images per edition as JPEG or PNG through the Airtable form, each file under 10 MB, 240 dpi acceptable and 300 dpi preferred for print; previously published work is eligible as long as you retain the right to republish, noted in the submission. There is no submission fee: the FAQ on the Issue 4 call page states "There is no submission fee. FRAME/SEQUENCE believes in removing financial barriers to participation in the arts community." Accepted contributors are paid a one-time cash award per accepted piece plus a royalty apportioned by level of contribution out of a shared royalty pool paid through the fiscal sponsor; artists keep full ownership and all rights and may keep using, selling or publishing the work, signing a publishing agreement by DocuSign if selected. Three inconsistencies sit on the organiser's own pages, and one of them is about money. The submissions FAQ still answers the deadline question with the inaugural issue's window (June 20 – July 18, 2025), while the dated Issue 4 block on both the submissions page and the homepage reads September 15 – November 15, 2026. The About page says twice that the periodical charges nothing to submit — "we do not charge a submission fee, and we pay our artists for having their work published" and "submissions are always free" — the live Issue 4 Airtable form carries no payment step, no fee line and no membership question, and as of 13 October 2026 the call page's own FAQ agrees: "There is no submission fee." And the team page calls the periodical quarterly where the homepage and submissions page call it semiannual.
