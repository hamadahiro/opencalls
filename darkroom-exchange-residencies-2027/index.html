Darkroom Exchange Residencies 2027

Prizes 15,000 NOK artist fee Residency

This is one of the few residency programmes built specifically around the darkroom rather than around a studio with a view. Twenty-three artists went through it between 2023 and 2024 and six in 2026, each travelling to a community darkroom in another Nordic country, so the exchange is the point: you work on your own project alongside the photographers who use that darkroom every week.

Read the darkroom listings before you apply, not after. The programme's own instruction is that each darkroom has its own equipment, community and surrounding landscape — several offer colour development and printing but not all of them — and you rank your preferences in the application form, so the choice is yours to get right. The periods differ sharply too, from three weeks at Island Darkroom in Scotland to eight at Mörk in Finland.

The money is a fee, not a budget line you draw on: 15,000 NOK (about €1,400), or 18,000 NOK if you choose a sustainable travel option, and it is expected to cover your travel and living expenses for a stay of three to eight weeks. Accommodation and the workspace are free, some darkrooms throw in chemicals or materials, and special materials you order through the darkroom you pay for yourself. Anything else is on you.

The sustainability theme is a condition of the exchange, not of your practice. The organisers are explicit that invited photographers do not have to work within a theme of sustainability — they are either offered a workshop on it or asked to teach one, and if they teach, they are paid a separate teacher's fee. Say in your work plan which of the two you want; some darkrooms are looking specifically for a teacher, others for a participant.

One structural restriction worth checking before you start: you must live in one of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway or Sweden (the Faroe Islands, Greenland and Åland count), and you must apply to a darkroom in a different country from the one you live in.

Neither the open call nor the network's own pages state any policy on AI-generated or AI-assisted work — unsurprising for a programme built on analog processes, but it is an absence rather than a rule.
