Verdant Fund Project Grant 2026

Prize Up to $7,000

Grants of up to $7,000, from a pool of $60,000 dispersed each year, for non-traditional, public-facing visual art projects made in Alabama by Alabama-based artists. The Verdant Fund is the state's Andy Warhol Foundation Regional Regranting programme, administered jointly by Alabama Contemporary Art Center, the Coleman Center for the Arts and Space One Eleven, and it funds nine to fourteen projects a year. Projects must be artist-driven, happen outside traditional venues such as museums, galleries and art centres, and include a public-facing component that engages the local community - the fund's own examples include billboards and other interventions in public space, site-specific installation in non-art venues, artist books, zines and short-run publications with free public access, socially engaged and civic-practice work, moving image or new media grounded in visual arts, and dialogue-driven practices such as podcasts, research and art journalism. No medium is enumerated, and the fund's own 2026 roster answers the question directly: three of the funded projects are photographic - Didimos Johnson Pulikkottil's Camp Hill, 2030, described as a documentary photography and storytelling project shown as an open-air exhibition across a rural town; Elizabeth Ward's Under the Magnolia Trees, preserving elders' stories through film, photography and audio; and Miriam Omura's The Missing Catalog, a mobile analog archive of long-term missing persons using film photography. What the fund does not support is work that is primarily narrative or documentary film, performing arts, product design or architecture, projects inside an institution's regular programming, and anything accessible only behind a paywall. The lead applicant must reside in Alabama and keep a permanent address in the state, and must have a professional documented practice; proposals from women artists, artists of colour, artists with disabilities, LGBTQ+ artists and artists working in or from rural Alabama are prioritised. Artist compensation is a stated goal - the fund publishes recommended honorariums and says projects without them will be less competitive. Applications are free, filed through Submittable with up to seven work samples, and reviewed by a panel of three jurors drawn from regional and national artists, curators and arts professionals; the three partner organisations do not select awardees. Applications opened August 10, close October 9, 2026, notifications go out in December 2026, and funded projects may begin from January 2027 and must be complete by December 2027.
