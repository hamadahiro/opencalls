53rd Annual Membership Exhibition

Prize Group exhibition

The Albrecht-Kemper Museum of Art's 53rd annual exhibition of work by its own members, on view in the museum's galleries in Saint Joseph, Missouri from 16 January to 21 February 2027, with an opening reception on Friday 15 January from 4 to 7pm and an awards ceremony at 6pm that evening. Photography is category 5 of the ten entry categories, alongside oil and acrylic painting, watercolour, drawing, printmaking, pottery and ceramics, 2-D mixed media, digital art, fibre and textiles, and sculpture and 3-D mixed media; museum staff may reassign a work to the category they think fits. The exhibition is judged, and the entry form carries an opt-out box for artists who do not want their work considered for awards. Entry itself is free, but the show is open only to current AKMA members — new members may join at any time before the drop-off period, with individual membership at $35 a year, $15 for students with a student ID, $20 for teachers and $25 for anyone living more than 60 miles from Saint Joseph. Each individual membership may submit two artworks; a family or dual membership may submit two per person but no more than three as a household. Work must have been made in the last five years, must not have been shown at the museum before, and is capped at 4 by 4 feet including the frame and 50 pounds, with works on paper matted and framed behind glass or Plexiglass and 2-D work wired ready to hang. There is no entry portal: the completed, signed paper entry form is handed in with the artwork at the museum's front desk during the drop-off window of 6 to 10 January 2027, and work is collected again on 22, 23 or 24 February. Artists may mark a work for sale or not; the museum retains 30% on anything sold during the exhibition. Nothing may be removed before the exhibition closes, the museum does not store work, and anything not collected within 30 days is subject to disposal. The Albrecht-Kemper opened as the Albrecht Gallery in 1966 in the former Albrecht family home, took its present name in 1991, and holds one of the Midwest's finest collections of 18th- through 21st-century American art.
