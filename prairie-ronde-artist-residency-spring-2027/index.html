Prairie Ronde Artist Residency — Spring 2027

Prizes $2,000 stipend $500 travel grant

Spring session of the residency at The Mill at Vicksburg, a former Lee Paper Company mill on eighty acres in the village of Vicksburg, Michigan, now a redevelopment project. The Spring 2027 session runs March 15 to May 31, with two to four residents in residence for five to six weeks. The programme does not limit the residency to any specific medium, so photographers are eligible alongside every other discipline; what it looks for is independent, engaged and curious artists who can creatively interact with The Mill and its surrounding property. Residents receive a $2,000 stipend on completion, a $500 travel grant, private housing, a gallery show in downtown Vicksburg, video documentation of their time there and a membership to the Mug Club at the Distant Whistle Brewery. In return, Prairie Ronde is process-based rather than product-based, and asks that each artist donate one piece made during the residency to its permanent collection. Applications open on September 1, 2026 and are due December 1, 2026, with selections announced January 15, 2027; there is a $25 application fee, which the organisers say covers SlideRoom costs and an honorarium for the review panellists. Applications are scored against a published rubric — the body of work, the statement of purpose, the plan for incorporating The Mill or Vicksburg, evidence of genuine research into the programme, the ability to thrive independently, and openness to living in a rural community — and the highest-scoring applicants are then interviewed by the Residency Director and Coordinator. One restriction to note before applying: since April 25, 2025 the programme has temporarily suspended its consideration of international applicants, so it is effectively open to US-based artists for now. Note also that the page states the stipend as $2,000 in its list of what the residency offers but refers to a $2,500 stipend in its answer about collaborative applications; the lower published figure is the one recorded here.
