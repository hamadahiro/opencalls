Not Too Much off the Top: Barbershops and Salons as Gathering Spaces

The curator wrote the brief as a memory, and it is a photographer's memory: clippers humming, the radio going, conversations running between the barbershop at the front and the salon at the back, a barber who knows exactly how you like your hair. Karrington Gardner calls the space "more than a place of personal maintenance" — "where relationships are nurtured, stories are exchanged, and community is maintained."

What makes this better than most theme shows is that the references are already argued. Gardner names Kerry James Marshall's De Style and School of Beauty, School of Culture, and then Devin Shimoyama's Mighty Mighty: The Barbershop Project, which "challenges the heteronormativity in the Black barbershop" and the isolation of being LGBTQ+ in it. A submission that only celebrates the subject is answering half the call.

Entry is free, up to three works, one to five images each. Five cash awards — $500, $300, $200 and two $100 honorable mentions — and a six-week show in the Arts Center Gallery in downtown Fayetteville from 22 January 2027. The jury is deliberately not all art-world: two painters and a working barber from Charlotte sit on it with the curator.

Practical limits are few and physical: 2D and 3D both accepted, anything wall-hung needs wire backing for the gallery's walker system, and the work has to fit through a standard door. Acceptances 6 January, delivery window 7–19 January. Artists must be 18 or older, and the call addresses Black artists of all identities in the diaspora.
