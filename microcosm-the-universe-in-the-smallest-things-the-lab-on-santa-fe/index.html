MICROCOSM: The Universe in the Smallest Things

An in-state call from The Lab on Santa Fe, a gallery at 840 Santa Fe Drive in Denver's Art District on Santa Fe whose exhibitions are seen by crowds during the district's First Friday Art Walks. The premise is that if you look closely enough at one small thing you start to see everything: a single mark, a strange little object, the texture of paint, the pattern of a leaf, the surface of a stone, the shifting light across a Colorado landscape. The organisers want work that zeroes in before it zooms out, and are explicit that this is not a nature-themed exhibition — the subject might be a landscape, a face, a room, a memory, an invented object or something that cannot be named at all. They say they love artists who trust the small moments instead of chasing the obvious ones, and ask whether obsession and compulsion can transmute into virtuosic brilliance. If your work rewards a closer look, it belongs in the conversation. The gallery publishes no list of eligible or excluded media, and it has previously hosted a dedicated international street photography exhibition, so lens-based work is in scope. The call is open to Colorado artists only, and applicants must be able to drive their work in for delivery — no shipping is allowed. Work can be turned away at drop-off for improper wiring. All submitted art must be for sale, at the price given on the application, and measurements on the application must be accurate. Entry is $29 for one or two images, one application per artist. The exhibition has been moved and lengthened: the gallery now runs it 1 to 30 January 2027, having upgraded it from its original three-week November slot.
