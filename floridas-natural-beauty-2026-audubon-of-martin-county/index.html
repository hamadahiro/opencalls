Florida’s Natural Beauty 2026

Prizes $300 Best in Show $200/$100/$50 per category Exhibition

Audubon of Martin County has been protecting birds and habitat on Florida Treasure Coast for over seventy years, and its annual photography contest is the rare chapter-level nature competition that ends in a real museum show rather than a web gallery. Winners and accepted work hang at the Elliott Museum in Stuart for five weeks, 22 January to 25 February 2027, after an opening reception and awards ceremony on 21 January. The contest is co-hosted by the Historical Society of Martin County, which runs the museum.

Entry is open to amateur and professional photographers residing in the US, but every photograph and video must have been taken in Florida. There are six categories — Birds, Wildlife, Flora/Landscape, Creative, Video and Cell Phone Photography — and you may enter up to seven images or videos per category, each image in one category only. The fee is per uploaded photograph: $25 for non-members, $20 for Audubon of Martin County members in good standing through the run of the show, and membership status is verified. Awards are Best in Show $300, and in each category first $200, second $100, third $50, with honourable mentions at the judges discretion. Judging is blind, by a panel of three independent photography experts, over the week of 2 to 9 December, with notifications on 11 December.

Two things to read carefully. First, this is not a digital-only exhibit — accepted images must be supplied as tangible museum-quality prints on photo, metallic or fine art paper, canvas, aluminium, wood or acrylic, minimum 8 inches on the short side, no more than 20 by 24 inches including the frame, wired for hanging with no sawtooth hangers, and out-of-area entrants ship prepaid round trip to Palm City. Budget for framing and shipping before you enter. Second, the processing rules are strict: outside the Creative category only global adjustments are permitted (lighter, darker, sharpening, overall contrast, saturation), and the Creative category — the one that allows masking, changed areas and added elements — states that the use of AI is not permitted. Camera-trap images are disqualified; the organisers want the photographer present in the field. Rights are clean: you keep copyright and grant only archival, publicity and promotional use, and sales split 75% to the artist, 25% to the museum.
