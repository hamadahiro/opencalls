Celebrating Realism

Prizes $200 NYC exhibition

Realism calls can be quietly hostile to photography - the assumption being that a camera does realism automatically, so there is nothing left to judge. MVA Gallery does not read it that way. The prospectus opens with Courbet's Realist Manifesto and its insistence that artists portray everyday people, activities and objects, that nature be depicted with its irregularities, and that idealised figures and romanticised fantasies be refused. It then traces what grew from that: Photorealism, Social Realism, New Objectivity, American Regionalism, Hyperrealism - movements the gallery describes as a rebellion against Abstractionism, still taking new forms and using current technologies.

The questions the call actually asks are craft questions, and they apply as squarely to a photographer as to a painter: how do you observe and record perceived reality, how do you organise pictorial elements, embrace precision, manipulate light and modify colour tonalities while keeping emotion in the work. There is no restriction on subject matter - landscapes, cityscapes, still lifes, events, special occasions, man-made environments, animals, people. The mediums line names photography alongside painting, watercolour, printmaking, mixed media, digital, pastel, encaustic, ink, sculpture, fibre and drawing, and the artwork specifications close with the sentence that matters: all photographic processes are welcome. There is no cutoff date for when the work was made.

MVA - Modern Visual Arts - is a physical gallery in the historic Moravian quarter of Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, an hour from New York City. Entry is $35 for three images and $5 for each additional image up to nine, open to US residents aged 18 and over, and jurying is by a small rotating committee of artists, gallerists, art reviewers, publishers and academics who have asked to remain anonymous for this exhibit. The award ladder is the one MVA runs across its programme: an Art of Distinction award carrying a featured exhibit in the gallery second room, an optional $200 Director Choice award, honourable mentions that come with free entry to a future MVA show, and an annual grand prize of one New York City exhibition opportunity drawn in December from everyone shown that calendar year. Work must be wired, no sawtooth hangers, under 48 inches framed and 50 lbs, with plexiglass preferred for anything shipped. The gallery takes 30% commission and artists pay shipping and insurance both ways with a prepaid return label enclosed. Entries close 30 September 2026, notification is on or about 20 January 2027, accepted work must arrive by 23 February, and the exhibition runs 28 February to 26 March 2027.
