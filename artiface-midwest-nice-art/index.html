art·i·face

art·i·face is a juried open call from midwest nice art, the Sioux Falls artist-run curatorial platform, open to artists internationally who work with materials in a surprising manner. Most utilitarian objects have a specific use; the brief asks how artists transform that purpose, citing Robert Rauschenberg's combines, El Anatsui's bottle-cap wall hangings, Ingrid Calame's paintings traced from forgotten stains on the street, and Rachel Whiteread's unconventional casting sculptures. Artists are asked to consider a secondary use or process within their work — jigs, templates, casts, moulds, scraps, detritus and found objects — used as a way of working, as subject matter, or as a route to abstraction. midwest nice art runs all-media themed shows, and the entry form asks only for the title, medium, size and year of each work; photography runs throughout the gallery's exhibition archive, whose credit lines include archival pigment prints, photographic collage and photographs on paper. The juror is Andrew Nordin, an artist and educator who teaches studio art and art history at Ridgewater College and curates its galleries; he holds BFAs in Painting and Graphic Design from St. Cloud State University and an MFA in Pictorial Arts from San José State University, received a 2017 Minnesota State Arts Board award, and recently completed a residency at Natthagen Art Center in Julussdalen, Norway. Up to three works may be submitted for a $5 exhibition fee, paid via Square once the entry form is completed. Images should be 300 dpi and 1500 px on the longest side. The online exhibition opens 15 December 2026. Students can enter free with the code STUDENT26, according to the organisers' 17 October announcement.
