2027 BAXTER ST Residency

Baxter St at the Camera Club of New York — one of the city's oldest not-for-profit arts organisations and a base for the contemporary lens-based community — invites emerging lens-based artists living in New York City who have yet to have a solo exhibition in the city to apply for its workspace residency. Residents get free use of Baxter St's facilities and equipment for the duration of the residency, a stipend of $1,000 a month across the three months of workspace access, a $750 materials fee towards film, printing and other production costs, a $3,000 production fee to realise the exhibition, and $165 per public programme. Each resident is granted a solo exhibition at Baxter St after their residency term, and takes part in the organisation's programming: a public conversation, a video interview and one-on-one meetings with an Art Advisory Committee member and Baxter St staff. The organisers read lens-based broadly — photographers, filmmakers, collage artists and others who use photography as a medium or a point of departure are all welcome. Applicants must be New York City residents for the duration of the residency period and 18 or older; living accommodation is not provided. Only lens-based work is considered, and Baxter St staff, board members, previous Workspace or Darkroom residents and their families are not eligible. Anyone enrolled in a degree-granting programme as of 1 January 2026 is ineligible, and students must have completed their studies on or before 1 January 2027. There is no fee to apply. The application, made through Submittable, asks for a work sample of ten images (installation shots allowed) plus up to one video of a maximum three minutes, a letter of intent of up to 300 words carrying an exhibition proposal, a one-paragraph artist statement of up to 300 words, a one-page CV and two references. The 2027 application cycle opened on 23 September 2026 and closes 25 November 2026 at 11:59pm ET; Baxter St states the deadline will not be extended, and a jury of art professionals considers every eligible application. The residency is supported by public funds from the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs in partnership with the City Council, the Joy of Giving Something, Inc., and the Howard and Sarah D. Solomon Foundation. Note on access: the ground-floor galleries and restroom at 154 Ludlow Street are wheelchair accessible, but the main resident workspace is one flight down and is not.
