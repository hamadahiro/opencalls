Beca Joana Biarnés 2026

Prizes €8,000 9-month mentoring Equipment loan

The eighth edition of the grant the Fundación Photographic Social Vision created in 2019 in honour of Joana Biarnés, Spain's first woman photojournalist, who asked before her death that the proceeds of her work be used to strengthen the future of photojournalism. It funds an in-depth photojournalistic investigation carried out inside Spanish territory on a subject of current social relevance. The winner — an individual or a team — receives €8,000 to carry out the work over a maximum of nine months, mentoring throughout from a tutor with extensive teaching and professional experience, the temporary loan of photographic equipment from Foto Ruano Pro, and a guarantee that the finished project will be published in whatever format suits it best. Up to three finalists, the winner included, each get a portfolio review with an expert of their choosing from the foundation's list of collaborators, free access to the narrative-journalism workshop run by the independent magazine 5W and three months of its online subscription. Applicants must be between 18 and 35 years old inclusive and tax-resident in Spain; applications are direct from the author or authors, one proposal each, and in collective projects every member must meet the conditions and one acts as spokesperson. The jury looks for an unpublished subject or a genuinely new perspective on a familiar one, a project still in its early stages with most of the photographic research ahead of it, and work that has not yet received any national or international prize; the outcome must include an extensive series of still photographs, with multimedia accepted only as a complement. Methodology counts: the proposal has to identify expert sources by name, and the funded investigation must include at least two interviews with relevant specialists. The application is entirely free and can be made in Spanish or in Catalan with a Castilian version attached, as a set of PDFs — a CV carrying the ID number, address and date of birth of each participant; a portfolio of at least 25 images of previous work, noting what has already been published; a project presentation with title, justification, motivation in no more than 200 words, methodology and sources, visual approach, and a nine-month schedule and budget; plus a portrait and three photographs of your own connected to the theme, for promotion if you are shortlisted. Everything is sent by WeTransfer or a Dropbox or Drive link, emailed from the applicant's own address. The call runs from 14 September to 16 November 2026. The eighth-edition jury is Bego Antón, Olmo Calvo, Gorka Lejarcegi, María Santoyo and Jordi Rovira. The winner grants the foundation non-exclusive reproduction rights for its own communications and must credit the grant in any publication of the work.
