Shoot The Frame 2026 Annual Portrait Awards

Prizes US$4,000 in cash and a ViewSonic ColorPro monitor for each winner Publication in the 2026 Annual Book

The second edition of Shoot The Frame's Annual Portrait Award, from a platform that has run portrait, landscape and wildlife awards since 2012 and replaced its monthly contests with a single annual in 2025. Entries opened 1 November 2026 and close 31 December at 11:59pm US Pacific; the organiser publishes a two-day extension in its own timeline, to 2 January 2027 at 11:59pm Pacific. Two categories: One Frame, for single portraits each judged on its own merit, and Photo Essay, a curated series of up to fifteen portraits judged as one unified entry. One Frame is priced as packs — 1 photo $17, 3 for $27, 7 for $37 — and the Photo Essay is $49 for up to fifteen images; there is no cap on how many entries one photographer may submit. Prizes total US$4,000 in cash across six places: each category awards a winner US$1,000 and two finalists US$500 each. Both winners also receive a ViewSonic ColorPro monitor, and the Photo Essay winner a lifetime Format portfolio website. Every winner, finalist and honourable mention is published in the 2026 Annual Book, a softcover A4 volume printed in March 2027. Judging takes place in January 2027 and runs anonymously — the judges are named publicly but photographers are not identified to them — with winners and finalists announced in February. The 2026 panel is Tristan Hewat (founder), Tomasz Trzebiatowski (founder and editor-in-chief of FRAMES magazine), photographer and graphic designer Ian Pettigrew, and brand representatives from the two prize partners, ViewSonic and Format. Entry is open globally to amateurs and professionals of any age, with parental permission required under 18. Files are JPG, 2MB–10MB, sRGB, minimum 2000 pixels on the shortest edge. Photographers retain copyright and submitted images are used only to promote the awards, always with credit. AI-generated images are not allowed, nor are composite images or major digital manipulations; general editing and post-processing are permitted. Past placed photographers include Javier Arcenillas and Trevor Cole.
