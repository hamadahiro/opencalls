William Flanagan Memorial Creative Persons Center — Winter/Spring 2027

Prizes $2,500 stipend Month-long residency

Edward Albee founded this residency in 1962 with the proceeds of Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf?, and the foundation has run it ever since at the William Flanagan Memorial Creative Persons Center - known to everyone as The Barn - a converted horse stable on a secluded knoll about two miles from the centre of Montauk and the Atlantic. After a renovation completed in the early 2020s it now operates nearly year-round and houses four people at a time, usually two visual artists and two writers, each with an en-suite bedroom and a separate private studio, plus a shared library, eat-in kitchen and patio. Residencies run for whole calendar months only; no partial stays, and no four-week block straddling two months. This application window covers residencies from January to June 2027 (the Barn closes every December). Selection is made, in the foundation's words, using only talent and need as criteria, and the call is open to any creative person over 18 of any nationality, students included. There is no application fee and no cost of any kind to stay. Domestic Fellows receive a $2,500 stipend on completing the residency; international Fellows are reimbursed for expenses up to the same amount. Visual arts applicants submit 6 to 12 images and/or up to three media files with a CV, an artist's statement setting out the proposed project, and the email addresses of two professional referees, whom the foundation contacts itself. Photography sits squarely inside the visual-arts stream: the current fellows include Becca Albee, whose work is anchored in photography, Robin Crookall, a NYSCA/NYFA Artist Fellow in Photography, and the lens-based artist Caroline Minchew, whose photographs are held in the National Gallery of Art Photograph Study Collection.
