The Gift of Art 2026

Hal Bromm established Tribeca's first contemporary gallery in 1975, showing early minimal and conceptual work by Carl Andre, Rosemarie Castoro, Victor Burgin and John Hilliard, and giving early solo exhibitions to Krzysztof Wodiczko, Keith Haring and David Wojnarowicz. The gallery shows photography as a matter of course: its summer 2026 exhibition photo·graph surveyed contemporary photographic practice with work by Bernd and Hilla Becher, Renate Aller, Slava Mogutin and others. Since the 1980s THE GIFT OF ART has been the gallery's annual holiday tradition, in which artists are invited to make unique, saleable artworks as ornaments to adorn the gallery tree - often with overtly political themes that challenged the usual sugar plums and Christmas angels. Last year's edition featured over 100 handmade ornaments by more than 60 artists from around the globe. For 2026 the gallery is expanding the show to include artist-made prints, so the work can live in a home year-round. Each artist may submit up to three ornaments and two prints, and may enter one or both categories. The print category is printmaking only: the gallery's list is wood block, lino, etching, lithography, silkscreen, editioned darkroom prints and monoprints, and it rules out reproductions and prints of digital artwork, so photographers enter with hand-printed darkroom editions rather than inkjet work. Prints must be signed, numbered and no larger than 16 by 20 inches; ornaments must hang on a tree, measure no more than 10 inches in any direction and be priced under $500. Neither form will consider work made by or with generative AI. Submissions are reviewed on a rolling basis; the gallery's page says artists are notified no later than 1 November 2026, while both submission forms still carry the pre-extension date of 10 October and ask for accepted work to reach the gallery by 1 November. Both ornaments and prints are for sale at the gallery throughout December, with 30% of proceeds going to Rehabilitation Through the Arts, which has run arts programming in New York prisons since 1996. The exhibition runs 1 to 19 December 2026.
