Interlace Project Grant 2026

Prize $6,000

Interlace Project Grants give Providence-based artists $6,000 to make and publicly present new visual work. The fund is Rhode Island's arm of the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts' Regional Regranting Program and is administered by Dirt Palace Public Projects; it exists to make visible often unseen and under-supported artistic activity by artists at all career stages, and to cultivate engaged communities around that work through collaboration in process or presentation. Fundable activities include a new body of visual artwork with a publicly accessible component, a public event, performance or intervention, a site-specific or public-facing art project, a new exhibition or exhibition series, a screening, lecture, broadcast or workshop series, publications in printed or digital form, and experimental forms. The fund will not support projects led by non-profits, incorporated businesses or students, applications made purely to rent studio space or to cover classes, travel or equipment, or large projects for which the grant would cover only a portion of the budget - Interlace expects to be the primary source of funding. Applicants must live within a 10-mile radius of Providence and in the state of Rhode Island (the radius does not extend into Massachusetts), be 18 or over, have been making visual art for at least two years, have resided in Rhode Island for at least the year before the deadline, not be full-time students, and not have completed a Project Grant as lead applicant in the past two years. Applications ask for contact information, visual support materials, a project title, synopsis and biographies, a project description addressing the fund's priorities of visual art, collaboration and engagement, and a budget; a group may apply but must name a lead applicant, who receives the funds and files the final report. An independent panel of national and local artists, curators and arts professionals ranks proposals on originality, clarity of application materials and commitment to those priorities, and the panellists are named when the awardees are announced. Applications opened July 13, an early-review deadline fell on August 16, and the final deadline is Sunday September 13, 2026 at 11:59pm EST; applicants are notified in November 2026 and grantees announced between December 2026 and January 2027. Free to apply, through Submittable.
