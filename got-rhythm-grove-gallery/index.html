Got Rhythm

Prize Exhibition

A themed group show at Grove Gallery on Grove Street in downtown Evanston, run by the Evanston art school Studio 215, asking how artists respond to rhythm, sound and music. The organisers want two-dimensional work that reverberates with the rhythm of life — heartbeat, breath, the cadence of speech — and are open to everything from abstraction to representation, citing Kandinsky's music-driven paintings and centuries of figure painters conveying the movement of dance. Accepted media are painting, drawing, printmaking, photography and textiles; work must hang on the wall, weigh under five pounds and measure no more than 30 inches on the longest side including the frame. Only original, one-of-a-kind works are accepted and open-edition giclée prints are excluded, so a photographer should be entering a print made as the work itself. This is a regional call, limited to artists living in Illinois, Indiana, Wisconsin, Ohio, Iowa, Kentucky or Minnesota. Entries are judged by Melanie P. Brown, a Chicago painter and teaching artist who has performed live painting with local musicians, and Ross Martens and Darren Oberto, both co-owners of Alley Gallery in Evanston — Martens studied Film and Photography at Northwestern and works as an artist, photographer and framer. The exhibition runs across September and October 2026 with an opening reception in early September, and is promoted across the Midwest in Chicago Gallery News, Newcity and elsewhere. All work must be for sale, split 60% to the artist and 40% to the gallery.
