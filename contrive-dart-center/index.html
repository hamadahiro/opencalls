Contrive: A National Exhibition of Manipulated & Reimagined Photography

Prizes $300 Exhibition

d'Art Center, the nonprofit art center founded in downtown Norfolk in 1986 and now anchoring the NEON District, gives its historical bank vault over to manipulated photography for five weeks. Contrive is a national juried exhibition of photographs that have been reimagined and transformed by physical or digital means — long and double exposures, compositing, hand embellishing, professional editing software — and the call is explicit that no AI-rendered images or components are permitted. The organisers frame it historically: photographers have always built their own worlds out of darkroom tricks and optical illusions, and the show asks what those peculiar worlds look like now. The Vault is a 132-square-foot space, so this is a competitive call — roughly 25 works will hang. Hang- or pedestal-ready work, frame included, must measure no more than 36 inches in its longest dimension, though the Gallery Director may waive that for artists who take part in installing; 2D work needs a wire. Entry is $30 for three submissions — three images is the minimum, so a single work may be entered three times or padded with detail shots — and each additional work is $5, up to a cap of ten. Gallery Director Amanda Bradley curates the selection; award winners are chosen by a panel of d'Art's professional artists and staff, with $300 for Best in Show. The exhibition runs October 31 to December 5, 2026, opening alongside the main-gallery show VIVID at a reception on November 6, and is archived permanently on d'Art's online archive. Sales carry a 60/40 split in the artist's favour, and artists arrange their own shipping, including pre-paid FedEx or UPS return labels.
