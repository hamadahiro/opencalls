Snapshot America: A Portrait of the United States in 2026

Prizes Exhibition Publication

The Rhode Island Center for Photographic Arts wants a time capsule of America at 250. This is a call to photograph the country as it is right now — your community, your friends, your neighbourhood, local events, landmarks, landscapes and the ordinary activities that make up daily life in the United States. From the grand celebrations to the tragedies that touch everyone, RICPA asks what image defines present-day America for you, what best represents the town you live in, what gives you the strength to get up in the morning and what keeps you up at night. Note the form RICPA wants: street photography, snapshots and other straight-out-of-camera work, with photo editing kept to a minimum. Curation is overseen by David DeMelim, RICPA's managing director, working with a team of educators, members and curators. Selected work hangs in the RICPA galleries at 118 North Main Street in Providence from 19 November to 11 December 2026, with an opening reception on the 19th, and an expanded collection — more work than the walls can hold — is published in book form as the lasting record. Deliberately, there are no individual prizes: to avoid implying that one community or one issue matters more than another, RICPA instead offers every entrant a discount coupon for the book. Entry is open to residents of all 50 states and the 14 territories, 18 and over, through CaFÉ. Current RICPA members get 20% off the entry fee, and membership can be bought at the point of entry. Accepted work must be exhibition-ready and no more than 48 inches including the frame; RICPA runs an optional print-and-frame-rental service from $45 for artists who would rather not ship. The gallery takes a 40% commission on sales, 20% for exhibiting and supporting members.
