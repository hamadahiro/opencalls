Photographs They Told You Not to Show

UNSTAGED is a small publisher whose books are built as “narrative-led anthological inquiries into comforting and discomforting authenticity”, and its next anthology, guest-curated by Aga Łuczakowska, goes after the pictures that were kept back. The call states its own brief without softening it: “This is an open call for projects that refuse to play nice. We are looking for heavy emotional impact, noise, Images that were once hidden, censored because they crossed a line. The ones that were ‘too much.’ Too intense. Too loud. Too intimate. Too honest.” The research question set for the book is who decides what is too much to be seen, and how those lines are drawn; the approach named is critical visual research, auto-theory and visual non-fiction. The publisher hands each volume to a guest artist who sets the open-call theme and serves as guest curator, guiding the publication to its final form; as with its previous projects, the selected works are gathered into an anthology book that explores the theme through photographic inquiry — the cover image shown with the call is from the first edition of the UNSTAGED Anthology, a photograph by Ben Osborne. There is no money in either direction: the call says “NO submission fee | NO contribution fee”, and no cash award, royalty or honorarium is mentioned — what is on offer is a place in a printed anthology. Entries are a set of 5 to 10 photographs as JPEGs with the long edge at 2000 pixels and 300 dpi (the publisher calls that resolution essential for print curation), with black-and-white images supplied in grayscale colour mode, plus a project description of up to 500 words. Files must be named Name_Project Title_01 and uploaded to a Google Drive folder set to public access, with the project description inside the folder, preferably as a PDF; the folder link is then sent to the publisher's own submission address. Emails with files attached directly are not considered. One clause is worth reading before writing the description: AI-assisted text editing is permitted, but the publisher says it will reject descriptions “dominated by generic AI language and lacking the artist's own autobiographical/auto-theoretical/research-based perspective”. That sentence is about the writing, not the photographs, and the call states no policy on AI-generated images. The deadline is December 6 at 23:59 AoE — Anywhere on Earth, the latest timezone on the planet — so the cutoff is a full day later than a UTC reading of the date suggests. Note that the publisher's own shop and open-calls pages at un-staged.com are behind a password at the moment, so the call post on its own Instagram account is the live surface for this edition.
